# MiniHouse · §13-5 radon, for a floor that is not on the ground

**State:** SCREENING — CONDITIONAL ON C-01. Not a radon assessment, not a site classification, not a compliance statement.

- Limit: annual average **≤ 200 Bq/m³**; pressure reduction must be available above **100 Bq/m³** (S023).
- The floor is **not on the ground**: a ventilated void over the chassis, **150–450 mm** deep (C-05 open).
- Cabin ventilation **90 m³/h**; the floor sees **7 Pa** of depression with the make-up air in place.
- Generated by `scripts/radon_check.py`; JSON companion: `knowledge/comparisons/radon.json`.

## 1. §13-5 asks two things, and this building answers them differently

The clause contains a **performance** requirement and a **prescriptive** route, and they are not the same question:

| | What it says | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| Performance | annual average ≤ **200 Bq/m³** | a concentration, which needs a site |
| Prescriptive | a **radon barrier against the ground**, plus preparation for pressure reduction above **100 Bq/m³**, *unless documented unnecessary* | an artefact this building does not obviously have |

The phrase that decides it is **"against the ground"**. This floor sits on a steel chassis with a ventilated void beneath it — `envelope_check` already models the void as a ventilated layer. So the prescriptive route describes a detail the building does not contain, and the question is whether the void *is* the measure or whether the barrier has to be built anyway.

**That question cannot be closed without a site.** What can be closed is how much margin the arithmetic leaves, and whether the answer is close enough to the limit for the site to matter.

## 2. The bound that makes this tractable

Radon moves into a building by **advection**, not diffusion: soil gas is pushed through openings by a pressure difference, and the radon rides with it. That has two consequences the project should record.

First, **a radon barrier is an airtightness item, not an Sd item**. The project's vapour barrier is specified as **Sd ≥ 10 m** — a diffusion number — and a membrane that satisfies it is not thereby a radon barrier. The two requirements are written in different currencies and one does not imply the other.

Second, the concentration has an upper bound that no void, no junction detail and no membrane can exceed. If E is the activity exhaled under the footprint and Q the cabin's ventilation rate, then even in the pathological case where **every atom exhaled under the floor reaches the room**:

> `C_cabin ≤ E / Q`

| Soil exhalation mBq/(m²·s) | E, Bq/h | Bound Bq/m³ | Margin to 200 | Margin to 100 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 324 | **3.6** | 56 × | 28 × |
| 20 | 1296 | **14.4** | 14 × | 7 × |
| 50 | 3240 | **36.0** | 6 × | 3 × |

The exhalation rates are a **generic order-of-magnitude range for ordinary ground**, not a source value and not project data. They are swept, and the conclusion is a statement about the bound rather than a prediction of a concentration.

**Even the top of that range leaves a bound of 36 Bq/m³ — 6× inside the limit and 3× inside the action level.** The margin is not a modelling result; it comes from the fact that 90 m³/h of ventilation passes through a footprint of 18 m².

## 3. The void, and what it actually buys

The void is a buffer volume: radon exhaled under the floor is diluted by the air changing through the perimeter before it can leak up into the cabin. How much it buys depends on whether the void is **open or skirted**:

| Void case | Driving pressure | Void air changes |
|---|---:|---:|
| open perimeter, 150 mm | 5.40 Pa | **864 /h** |
| open perimeter, 300 mm | 5.40 Pa | **432 /h** |
| open perimeter, 450 mm | 5.40 Pa | **288 /h** |
| skirted, 5 mm gap, 150 mm | 0.12 Pa | **32 /h** |
| skirted, 5 mm gap, 300 mm | 0.24 Pa | **23 /h** |
| skirted, 5 mm gap, 450 mm | 0.36 Pa | **19 /h** |

Those air-change rates look implausibly large, and they are the correct consequence of a shallow volume rather than an error: a 150 mm void over 18 m² holds 2.7 m³, so a wind-driven flow through 0.36 m² of perimeter gap turns it over hundreds of times an hour. The physical reading is simpler than the number — **an open void is not a buffer at all, it is outdoor air**.

The case that matters is therefore the **skirted** one, which is also the realistic one: a cabin in Bergen will want a skirt for wind and snow. Even then the void changes over **19–32 times an hour** — a residence time of two to three minutes — because the buoyancy pressure across a shallow void is small but so is the volume it has to move. At 300 mm that pressure is **0.24 Pa**.

That is the geometric reason the buffer is robust. Across every combination swept, the worst cabin concentration is **1.8 Bq/m³** (skirted, 5 mm gap, 150 mm, 30 % floor leakage) — against a bound of 36 Bq/m³ that the void can only improve on.

**So the void is not what makes this safe.** The bound already does. The void is worth having, and the design must not destroy it — a sealed skirt, or filling the void with insulation, would remove the buffer and leave the bound as the answer. That is a constraint to record now, because it is the kind of thing a later decision removes without noticing.

## 4. Where the conclusion stops: alum shale

The bound is only as good as the exhalation rate under the floor. On ordinary ground the limit is reached at **278 mBq/(m²·s)** and the action level at **139 mBq/(m²·s)** — both far above the swept range.

**Alum shale is the exception.** At **300 mBq/(m²·s)** the bound is **216 Bq/m³**, which exceeds the limit. Alum shale is real in Norway and its presence is a site fact, not a design fact.

That is why **C-01 decides this question and the arithmetic does not.** The screen can say the margin is large on ordinary ground and that it disappears on alum shale. Whether this cabin stands on either is a site classification nobody has made.

## 5. Where the barrier goes, if it is built

The prescriptive route is not dismissed here, and it should not be. Until a site measurement exists, the barrier stands — but it must be understood as a **floor-deck airtightness requirement**, not a ground membrane and not an Sd value.

The consequence is a detail, and it is 18 m long. The barrier has to be continuous across the **floor-to-wall junction**, which is the same corner [the 2D screen](verificacion-puente-2d.md) solved for heat and the one place where the steel crosses the line. A membrane that stops at the wall's inner face and restarts outside the chassis is two membranes with a gap, and the gap is the path.

The same corner therefore carries three requirements at once — the wrapping that brings f_Rsi back to 0.921, the vapour barrier's continuity, and the radon path. **One drawing answers all three, or none of them is answered.** [The junction detail](planos/detalle-encuentro-R0.svg) is that drawing: it is generated from the solver's constants, and it shows the barrier turning the corner at (13, −22) and the chassis plate crossing that plane when the beam is not wrapped.

## 6. The penetrations

A radon barrier is only as good as its holes, and this project has an unusual number of them by design:

| Penetration | Does it cross the radon line? |
|---|---|
| make-up air inlet | **no** — it enters the wall above the floor, and must not be routed under the deck |
| stove combustion air | **no** if taken through the wall; **yes** if drawn from the void |
| drain | **yes** — the one wet penetration through the floor |
| cooker-hood and bathroom extract | **no** — they leave through the wall or roof |

The make-up air screen's inlet is therefore compatible with the barrier, which is not obvious and is worth stating: **the hole the project has just sized is in the other membrane.** The drain is the one that needs a sealed collar, and the stove's combustion air becomes a barrier question the moment it is taken from under the deck instead of through the wall.

## 7. Limits

- **Not a radon assessment.** No site, no measurement, no soil classification, no concentration claim. Everything here is a bound on an assumed exhalation rate.
- The exhalation range is generic. It is the single input the conclusion is most sensitive to, and the alum-shale case shows what happens when it is wrong.
- The void height is a **C-05 unknown** and is swept. No chassis profile exists.
- The floor's share of the envelope leakage is swept, not measured.
- The void is treated as well mixed. A stratified void would dilute less near the floor, which the bound already covers.
- §13-5's prescriptive route is **not** discharged. The barrier and the preparation for pressure reduction remain required until C-01.
- Nothing here is a compliance statement, a site approval or a design release.
