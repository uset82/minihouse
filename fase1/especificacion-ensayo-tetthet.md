# MiniHouse · the airtightness test: specified, and not performed

**State:** SPECIFICATION — **NO MEASUREMENT EXISTS**. This is a plan to measure, not a measurement. It does not close §14-3's airtightness requirement and must not be reported as doing so.

- Requirement **n50 ≤ 1.5 h⁻¹ at 50 Pa** (§14-3, [S011](../knowledge/sources/S011.md)).
- Internal volume **36.92 m³**; envelope **66.48 m²**; **Q50 = 55.4 m³/h** at the limit.
- Target **1.30 h⁻¹**; acceptance **1.36** measured, so a 10 % uncertainty still clears 1.5.
- Standard **NS-EN ISO 9972:2015** — **not held** ([S031](../knowledge/sources/S031.md)).
- Generated by `scripts/tetthet_test.py`; JSON companion `knowledge/comparisons/tetthet.json`.

## 1. Why this document exists, and what it is not

The [ytelsesoversikt](ytelsesoversikt.md) calls row 14.5 the cheapest in the document, and it is: a pressure test on an 18 m² building is one fan and one afternoon. But *cheap* and *available* are different things, and this is where they separate. **You cannot pressure-test a building that does not exist.** The afternoon is not this week; it is the end of fabrication, and again at handover. So the row stays `undetermined` and what changes is only that the work is now specified rather than named.

That is worth being blunt about, because the temptation is to call the row closed. A specification is to a measurement what a screening is to a verification: the same shape, one step short — and this project has already been bitten once by confusing the two.

## 2. The number, and the geometry it is attached to

n50 is a **volume-normalised** quantity, and this building carries an unusual amount of envelope per unit of volume.

| Quantity | Value | How |
|---|---:|---|
| Internal volume V | 36.92 m³ | 18 m² × mean height 2051 mm |
| Envelope area A_E | 66.48 m² | walls and ceiling 48.48 + floor 18.00 |
| A_E/V | **1.80 m⁻¹** | the number that makes the limit hard |
| Q50 at the limit | **55.4 m³/h** | 1.5 × V |
| q50 at the limit | **0.83 m³/(h·m²)** | Q50 / A_E |
| Linear detail | 61.10 m | floor, eave, corners, opening reveals |
| Joint density | **0.92 m/m²** | joints / A_E |
| Leakage per metre at 50 Pa | **0.91 m³/(h·m)** | Q50 / joint length |

A compact two-storey dwelling reaches A/V ≈ **0.77** and a single-storey one ≈ **1.17**; this cabin is **1.80**. For the same n50 it must therefore hold a q50 **2.3×** lower than the two-storey house and **1.5×** lower than the single-storey one.

In absolute flow the target is trivial — 55 m³/h is a bathroom fan. In relative terms it is not: **0.83 m³/(h·m²) across 66.5 m² of envelope carrying 61 m of linear detail, or 0.91 m³/h per metre of joint at 50 Pa.**

That is the whole reason the test is worth buying. The number is easy to reach and easy to miss, and which one happened is not knowable from the drawings.

## 3. The standard, and the fact that the project does not hold it

The method is defined by **NS-EN ISO 9972:2015**, which replaced NS-EN 13829. The project has **no copy** ([S031](../knowledge/sources/S031.md), recorded *to acquire*), so this document does **not** quote its clauses, its number of measurement points, its pressure range or its uncertainty treatment. Those are properties of a document nobody here has read, and reconstructing them from memory is the exact failure the knowledge rules exist to prevent.

What can be said without it:

- The standard defines **more than one test method**, and they do not give the same number. A test of the envelope with the deliberate openings sealed is not the same measurement as a test of the building as used. **The method must be named on the certificate**, because a number without its method is not reproducible — and §2-3(1)'s guidance requires the evidence to be *verifiable in the project*.
- **The project needs both.** §14-3's n50 is a property of the envelope, so the envelope test is the one that answers the regulation. The ventilation and radon screens need the leakage the extract actually works against, which is the in-use state.
- The in-use number is **the larger of the two wherever an intentional opening stands open in use**. This building's inlet is closable, so the gap between the two methods is set by how the inlet is left — which is the same control problem [the make-up air screen](verificacion-aporte-aire.md) ends on. Where every opening is closable and closed, the two methods converge. An envelope-test pass therefore does **not** by itself demonstrate an in-use pass, and if only one method can be run it must be the in-use one, because it bounds the other.

**Recommendation, to be confirmed when the standard is acquired:** envelope test for the §14-3 figure, in-use test recorded alongside, both with the method named.

## 4. Two tests, not one — and the first one is the one that matters

| Test | When | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | after the barrier and the lining battens, before the interior boards | **locate** leaks |
| **T2** | at completion, before handover | **count** them |

A single test at completion tells you a number. Only T1 can tell you **where**, because once the boards go on the barrier is invisible — which is precisely the case §2-4's guidance names for photographs: *bygningsdeler som bygges inn og er vanskelig tilgjengelige*. This project's two load-bearing details are in that category: the wrapped steel at the floor edge, and the barrier's turn at (13, −22) in [the junction detail](planos/detalle-encuentro-R0.svg). Both are photographed, and both are only reachable at T1.

If only one test can be afforded, **T1 is the one that can still change the building**; T2 is the one the FDV file needs. At one fan and an afternoon each, the choice should not arise.

## 5. Acceptance, and why the target is not 1.5

The limit is **absolute**. The [declaration of margins](declaracion-de-margenes.md) records that a numeric level is not a tolerance unless one is granted in a forskrift or a vedtak, and none is. A measurement of 1.52 h⁻¹ is a **deviation** needing dispensation under PBL ch. 19, not a rounding.

But a measurement is not exact either, and the declaration's own rule applies unchanged: **a margin is adequate only if it is positive and larger than its own uncertainty.** Applied to a measurement, that rule moves the acceptance threshold below the limit:

| Test quality | Relative uncertainty (k = 2) | n50 that still demonstrates ≤ 1.5 |
|---|---:|---:|
| favourable — calibrated plate, still air | 5 % | **1.43** |
| typical field test | 10 % | **1.36** |
| poor — wind, one point per level | 20 % | 1.25 |

So the acceptance criterion is **measured n50 ≤ 1.36 h⁻¹ with a declared 10 % uncertainty**, and the construction target is **1.30**.

**1.30 is not a round number pulled from the air.** It is a target chosen to clear a typical test (1.36) while staying clear of the fault case in §6 (1.19) — and a poor test would fail it, which is an argument about the test rather than about the building. **The quality of the measurement is part of the acceptance**: a 20 % test can fail a cabin that was built to target, and the way to avoid that is to buy a good test, not a tight cabin.

## 6. The lower edge, and the two requirements that pull apart

§14-3 rewards a tighter envelope. The project's answer to §13-1(1) — a mechanical extract — needs to be **fed**. In a sealed cabin the only supply is leakage, so the depression the fan must hold *rises* as the envelope improves:

| n50 h⁻¹ | Q50 m³/h | Basic extract (90 m³/h), sealed cabin | Spare to the 150 Pa ceiling |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.50 | 55.4 | 106 Pa | +44 Pa |
| 1.40 | 51.7 | 117 Pa | +33 Pa |
| 1.30 | 48.0 | 132 Pa | +18 Pa |
| 1.20 | 44.3 | 149 Pa | +1 Pa |
| 1.19 | 43.9 | 151 Pa | −1 Pa |

The bottom two rows are the finding. **The envelope has a floor as well as a ceiling**, and the floor is **1.19 h⁻¹** at a 150 Pa fan and a 90 m³/h basic extract: at 1.20 the spare is +1 Pa, and at 1.19 it is negative — **the fan stalls** and the extract cannot be delivered at all, so the make-up inlet becomes the only supply.

A **1 Pa** spare at 1.20 h⁻¹ is not a margin. It is smaller than any plausible uncertainty in a fan curve, so by the declaration's own rule the row is not demonstrated even though the arithmetic says *deliverable*.

That floor is **not a requirement**. With the inlet working, the pressure is held at 10 Pa and n50 does not enter at all. It is a **fault case**, and it is the reason the inlet must be interlocked with the extract rather than left to the occupant: at 1.50 h⁻¹ a closed inlet leaves the cabin at 106 Pa; at 1.20 it leaves it at 149 Pa, which is the fan's ceiling and, per [the make-up air screen](verificacion-aporte-aire.md), the flue's reversal.

**So «as tight as we can build» is not the instruction.** The instruction is 1.30, and it is a band, not a minimum.

## 7. What the inlet costs across the band — almost nothing

The inlet is sized by the design extract, not by the leakage, so it barely moves as the envelope improves:

| n50 | Leakage at 10 Pa m³/h | Inlet flow m³/h | Area cm² | Diameter mm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.50 | 19.5 | 88.5 | 100.4 | 113 |
| 1.40 | 18.2 | 89.8 | 101.9 | 114 |
| 1.30 | 16.9 | 91.1 | 103.4 | 115 |
| 1.20 | 15.6 | 92.4 | 104.8 | 116 |

**The inlet can be ordered before the envelope is measured**, because its size does not depend on the result — 113 mm at the limit, 116 mm at the target's lower neighbour. That is a procurement consequence worth having: the one component the test might have been thought to gate is the one it does not.

## 8. The test measures two numbers, and the project assumes both

The screens use n50 **and** the leakage exponent n = 0.65. The test returns both — n from the slope of the log-log fit, which is why the points must span a range rather than cluster:

| Assumed exponent n | Basic extract at 1.5 h⁻¹ |
|---:|---:|
| 0.50 | 132 Pa |
| 0.65 (assumed) | 106 Pa |
| 0.80 | 92 Pa |

A **40 Pa** spread on the ventilation screen's headline number, from an assumption nobody has tested. The measurement replaces it in the ventilation, make-up air and radon screens at once.

## 9. What a result does to the other three screens

- **Ventilation** — the sealed-case pressures scale as Q50^(1/n), so a measured n50 replaces the 1.5 in all three rows of that screen's pressure table, and a measured n replaces the exponent that produced them.
- **Make-up air** — the inlet area is nearly insensitive (§7), but its *justification* changes: at 1.50 h⁻¹ the inlet is advisable, at 1.20 it is mandatory.
- **Radon** — the floor's inward leakage is a share of the envelope leakage at the floor's pressure, so a tighter envelope reduces the delivered radon as well as the depression. That is the one place where tighter is unambiguously better, and it is why the bound `C ≤ E/Q` survives either result.

## 10. After a move

§4-2 requires the owner to keep the documentation current «ved endringer i bruksforutsetninger, eller ved fysisk utførelse». A module that is lifted, transported and reconnected meets that condition at every deployment: the envelope travels intact, but every installation connection is remade. **The test is therefore a per-deployment record, not a one-off**, and the FDV file carries the T2 certificate of the most recent one.

Whether a move can change n50 at all is a question the project cannot answer without a second measurement. The honest position: **the first re-test is also the experiment.**

## 11. Limits

- **No measurement exists.** This document specifies a test; it does not report one, and §14-3's airtightness remains undischarged. Row 14.5 of the ytelsesoversikt stays `undetermined`.
- **The standard is not held.** No clause number, point count, pressure range or uncertainty rule is quoted from it; everything that depends on it is marked to be confirmed on acquisition.
- The uncertainty bands (5 / 10 / 20 %) are **stated, not derived**, and the acceptance threshold moves with them — that sensitivity is tabulated rather than hidden.
- The 150 Pa fan ceiling is the ventilation screen's generic range, not a selected product.
- The floor of 1.19 h⁻¹ inherits the 90 m³/h basic extract and the 0.65 exponent; both are assumptions until measured.
- The envelope area is the internal face area, which is where the barrier sits. External areas would give a lower q50 and a flattering comparison.
- Nothing here is a compliance statement, a design release or a fabrication instruction.
