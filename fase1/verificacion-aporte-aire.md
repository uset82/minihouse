# MiniHouse · the make-up air, its size, and the flue it cannot rescue

**State:** SCREENING — CONDITIONAL ON C-01. Not a ventilation design, not a product selection, not a compliance statement.

- Internal volume **36.9 m³** at the reference section.
- Extract basic **90 m³/h**, boosted one room **108 m³/h**. With no deliberate inlet the cabin sits at **106 Pa** on the basic rate and **140 Pa** on the boosted one.
- The inlet is sized for the boosted case, which is when the cabin is under the most extract and when the flue matters most.
- Orifice law Q = 3600 · Cd · A · sqrt(2 · dp / rho), Cd **0.6**, air **1.2 kg/m³**.
- Generated by `scripts/aporte_aire.py`; JSON companion: `knowledge/comparisons/aporte-aire.json`.

## 1. What this screen is for

[The ventilation screen](verificacion-ventilacion.md) established that a fan in a sealed cabin does not move air, and ended on a decision rather than a number: the make-up air has to come from somewhere. That decision has a size, and the size is the whole of the design.

It also left declaration row 14 at **−130 Pa** with no priced remedy. This screen prices the remedy, and then tests whether the price buys the row.

## 2. The hole, sized

A deliberate inlet of area A passes `Q = 3600 · Cd · A · sqrt(2 · dp / rho)`. Holding the cabin at a chosen depression while the design extract of **108 m³/h** runs fixes A, and A fixes the diameter:

| Design depression Pa | Leakage m³/h | Inlet m³/h | Area cm² | Diameter mm | Standard size? | Holdable in wind? |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|:--:|:--:|
| 2 | 6.8 | 101.2 | 256.5 | 181 | **no** | **no** |
| 5 | 12.4 | 95.6 | 153.3 | 140 | **no** | **no** |
| 10 | 19.5 | 88.5 | 100.4 | 113 | yes | yes |
| 15 | 25.3 | 82.7 | 76.6 | 99 | yes | yes |
| 20 | 30.5 | 77.5 | 62.1 | 89 | yes | yes |

"Standard size" means an inlet no larger than **125 mm** across; beyond that it is a duct, with its own fire, acoustic and barrier consequences. "Holdable" means a depression at least as large as the pressure a **3 m/s** breeze puts on a wall — **5.4 Pa**. Below that the wind sets the cabin pressure, not the extract, and the design point is a fiction.

The recommended inlet is therefore **113 mm** at **10 Pa** of design depression: it holds the boosted case at **10 Pa** and lets the basic case fall to **7 Pa**, removing **98 Pa** of the **106 Pa** the basic extract would otherwise carry.

The lowest buildable depression is the right choice, not the smallest hole: the hole costs nothing that scales, while the pressure is what harms the flue and the radon barrier. Choosing the smallest inlet would pick the highest pressure and call it efficiency.

That is the ventilation solved, and it is one wall valve.

## 3. The flue it cannot rescue

The same table, read against the stove instead of the extract:

| Design depression Pa | Flue margin, worst Pa | Flue margin, best Pa | Adequate by §2-2(3)? |
|---:|---:|---:|:--:|
| 2 | +8 | +18 | yes |
| 5 | +5 | +15 | **no** |
| 10 | 0 | +10 | **no** |
| 15 | −5 | +5 | **no** |
| 20 | −10 | 0 | **no** |

Adequacy here is the declaration's own test, applied to the worst end of the band: the margin must be **positive and larger than its uncertainty**. The draught is quoted as 10–20 Pa, so its half-width is 5 Pa, and the worst margin has to beat both 0 and 5.

**One of the five rows passes it, and 3 rows are buildable.** The two sets do not intersect. The only depression that passes the test is **2 Pa**, which needs a **181 mm** inlet — larger than the size limit, and below the pressure a 3 m/s breeze puts on the same wall. The inlet cannot hold it, so the row is not a design point, it is arithmetic.

**That conclusion does not depend on the 125 mm size limit.** Relax it and the 2 Pa row becomes a standard size, but it is still below the wind's own pressure, so the sets stay disjoint. The size limit only decides how much of the table is buildable; the wind decides that none of the adequate rows are.

The reason is structural rather than numerical. **A passive inlet cannot hold a set pressure**, because the wind's own pressure is larger than any design value that would help the flue. The inlet is sized by the extract and the weather does the rest.

## 4. Row 14 is retired, not improved

A **room-sealed** stove takes its combustion air directly from outside and its flue is balanced against the outside, so the cabin's pressure no longer appears in the draught at all. That removes the coupling instead of shrinking it, and it is a procurement requirement with no dimensional cost — which makes it the only thing in this project that closes a negative safety margin for free.

**Row 14 therefore does not become positive; it stops being a margin.** The declaration must carry it as a requirement on the appliance, not as a number to be met by the envelope, and no make-up air design substitutes for it. That is a stronger statement than the screen can make about any other negative row, and it is worth having: the project's worst number has a fix, and the fix is a specification.

## 5. What the hole costs in heat

The make-up air arrives at outdoor temperature and has to be brought to room temperature. At the basic extract, over a 30 K design difference:

| Case | Tempering load W | As a multiple of the fabric loss |
|---|---:|---:|
| no heat recovery | **905** | 1.65 × |
| 80 % recovery | **181** | 0.33 × |

The fabric loss it is compared against is **18.3 W/K** — the §14-3 limits applied to the reference section's element areas — so **550 W** over the same 30 K.

**The ventilation air costs 1.6 times the fabric.** In an 18 m² cabin that is not a line item, it is the design: without recovery the make-up air is the largest heat load in the building, and a heat-recovery unit is not an upgrade but a precondition for the extract rate being defensible.

This number belongs to the energy frame, which the project has not built. It is recorded here because it is a consequence of the ventilation decision, and because a **905 W** ventilation load in an 18 m² cabin is the kind of figure that should change a specification before it is discovered later.

## 6. What the hole does to the barrier

The obvious fear is that a hole in the vapour barrier defeats the moisture strategy. By diffusion it does not. Conductance is area over Sd, the barrier is **Sd 10 m** over **48.5 m²**, and the hole is **264 mm** of plain air — a factor of **38** in the hole's favour per unit area. The **100 cm²** inlet therefore carries **0.8 %** of the barrier's diffusion conductance.

**A fraction of one per cent is not the problem.** The problem is that this ratio counts diffusion only. An opening is a convective path, and convective moisture transport through a defect carries far more vapour than diffusion — the same sentence [the 2D junction screen](verificacion-puente-2d.md) uses to explain what a conduction model cannot see. No Sd calculation reaches it, and neither does this one.

So the inlet is **a control problem, not a diffusion problem**. It must be open when the extract runs and closed when it does not, which means a flap and a seal, not a bigger vapour resistance. The barrier penetration is unavoidable and must be a designed, closable, vapour-tight valve — and that is a different artefact from the one the moisture strategy has been assuming.

## 7. What it does for §13-5

Sustained negative pressure is the mechanism that draws soil gas through the floor. The inlet reduces it from **106 Pa** to **7 Pa**, a factor of **14.9**.

That is a real mitigation and it does not assess radon. §13-5 still requires a concentration, and the barrier and the preparation for pressure reduction are still unbuilt. What changes is that the mitigation now has a size, and the size is one wall valve rather than a mechanical supply system.

## 8. Limits

- **Not a ventilation design.** No duct is sized, no fan selected, no valve chosen, no location fixed. This is one opening's area against one pressure.
- Cd 0.6 is the optimistic end. A louvred valve with a filter and a flap is lower, which makes the hole **larger** than tabulated, not smaller.
- The leakage law is the ventilation screen's: n = 0.65, generic, unmeasured.
- The wind is treated as a static pressure. Real wind is gusty, and a gust on the intake face pressurises the cabin and reverses the flow through the inlet — which is a second reason a passive inlet is a control problem.
- The flue draught 10–20 Pa is an assumption range, not a source value, and the adequacy verdict inherits its uncertainty.
- The fabric loss is the §14-3 limits on internal-face areas. It is a denominator for comparison, not an energy-frame result.
- Nothing here is a compliance statement, a product selection or a design release.
