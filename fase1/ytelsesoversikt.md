# MiniHouse · ytelsesoversikt under TEK17 §2-2(4)

**State:** SCREENING — §2-2(4) OVERVIEW, NOT A COMPLIANCE STATEMENT. Every status below is what a screening report shows at R0. No value is a measurement and none is released.

§2-2(4) requires the documentation to describe **how the building is to be formed and which ytelser are relied on**, and to give a basis sufficient for detailed design. The veiledning ([S030](../knowledge/sources/S030.md)) prescribes the overview's content:

> ytelser gitt i forskrift · preaksepterte ytelser som er brukt · ytelser som er dokumentert ved analyse

**The object has not existed until now.** §2-2(4) has been recorded as unsatisfied until R2 is chosen, which is true of the *basis* — the section does not close, so the basis cannot yet be sufficient — but was never true of the object. The material has been in fifteen screening reports since the first week. This is it, assembled, with each value read from the screen that owns it rather than retyped.

## 2. Preaksepterte ytelser som er brukt — 0

**Empty, and the emptiness is a finding rather than an omission.** Every number this project relies on is either one the forskrift states or one a solver produced; none comes from the preaccepted catalogue. That is unusual, and it follows from the building: the preaccepted route is written for a house with a foundation, a heated envelope assembled on site and a normal roof, and this module has none of those three in the form the veiledning assumes.

It also has a consequence worth stating plainly. The veiledning warns that «det er **ikke tilstrekkelig** kun med en bekreftelse på at de preaksepterte ytelsene er fulgt» — a bare confirmation is not enough. Here there is nothing to confirm, so **every** functional requirement in this building is carried by an analysis or by a stated number, and the analysis route is not optional anywhere.

## 5. What the overview says about the building

**38 rows: 27 stated in the forskrift, 0 preaccepted, 4 documented by analysis, 7 outside the overview.** Of the **31** that are ytelser: **0 are met**, 9 are not met by the screening, 20 are undetermined and 2 do not apply.

**Not one ytelse is discharged, and that is what R0 means.** It is not a defect of the overview: a screening is not a verification, and the two rows that would read as met — smoke detection and the surface class — are specifications nobody has written yet, so they are undetermined too. What this document shows is the shape of the problem, not the state of the building, and it will keep showing that until something is built or measured.

**The queue is longer than the list looked.** 21 distinct decisions close these rows, and **17 of them close exactly one row**. The comfortable version — «four decisions unblock the building» — is false. C-01 closes 4, product closes 3, R2 closes 3, and the rest are one-offs. That is the thing assembling the overview adds: each screen looks tractable alone, and together they show a project whose remaining work is mostly individual, with two real levers — the site (**C-01**) and the section (**R2**).

**Three findings follow that the individual screens could not show.**

1. **The preaccepted category is empty, and that is structural.** A building with no foundation, no site-assembled envelope and no conventional roof has no preaccepted route to follow, so §2-2(2)(b)'s analysis route is not one option among two — it is the only route this project has. That raises the stakes on §2-2(3) rather than lowering them: there is no fallback in which a checklist substitutes for an argument.
2. **Two rows do not apply, and both for the same reason.** §13-3's radon barrier «against the ground» and §9-2's product route both describe a building assembled on its own site from products with manufacturers. This one is lifted onto a chassis and skinned in timber nobody can certify. The pattern is worth naming: **the prescriptive routes assume a foundation and a supply chain**, and where both are missing the regulation does not become inapplicable — it becomes an analysis.
3. **One row waits on work rather than on a decision, and even that work waits on the building.** §15-5's water installation has no design and could be drawn this week. §14-5's airtightness looked like the other one, and writing its test specification ([especificacion-ensayo-tetthet.md](especificacion-ensayo-tetthet.md)) showed that the *test* is cheap and the *building to test* is not: the measurement happens at the end of fabrication and again at handover. So the cheapest row in this document is cheap in money and not in time, and the earlier reading — that it waited on an afternoon — was too generous. **A specification is not a measurement**, which is the same distinction as a screening not being a verification.

## 6. What §2-2(4) still requires

- **The basis must be sufficient for detailed design**, and it is not: the section does not close at 20° under 4.00 m, so R2 is the step that produces the basis. This overview is the *inventory* the basis will be built from, not the basis.
- **Drawings may be required**, and the veiledning names fire safety as the example. One drawing exists — [the junction detail](planos/detalle-encuentro-R0.svg) — and it is conceptual. No fire-strategy drawing, no plan at 1:50 and no section at 1:20 exist.
- **The overview must be reissued when the geometry moves**, for the same reason the declaration is versioned: a ytelsesoversikt that describes a superseded geometry is worse than none, because it looks current.

## 7. Limits

- Every status is a screening status. **None is a verification, an approval or a compliance statement**, and the municipality decides which ytelse applies in a real case.
- Values that a screen owns are read from that screen's constants; values no script owns cite the card or report in the evidence column of the JSON companion. The self-test fails if a row loses its evidence or if a declared margin is not cited somewhere in this overview.
- The category assignment is this project's reading of §2-2(2), applied row by row. It is not a legal opinion.
- `§12-7(2)(d)` is a **determination**: parts of a room may be lower than 2.2 m where it does not affect the room's use, and how much is argued rather than measured. It is in §4 for that reason, and the §11-13 determination that governs rows 2–4 of the declaration appears as the closure of row 11.3 rather than as a ytelse of its own.
