## 1. What the declaration asked for and did not do

The declaration defines a margin as *value available − value required*, **accompanied by the uncertainty of the model that produced it**, and calls a margin adequate only when it is positive **and larger than that uncertainty**. It then says, in §8, that the uncertainty column is not populated for most rows.

That is the last open item of §2-2(3), and it is not decoration. A margin of −0.047 W/m²K and a margin of −0.047 ± 0.14 are different statements, and only one of them tells you whether to act. This screen populates the column.

## 2. Four kinds of uncertainty, because they behave differently

| Kind | What it is | How it behaves | Can it be swept? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **model** | the idealisation's own error — a 1D temperature field, a generic Ψ table, a uniform framing fraction | often **one-sided**: the idealisation is optimistic or pessimistic, not random | no — needs a finer model |
| **input** | a parameter nobody has measured — λ, LT, the timber fraction | roughly **symmetric**: the margin could be better or worse | yes |
| **interpretation** | which requirement applies | not an error at all — a determination | priced as alternative readings |
| **construction** | what the builder will actually achieve | a tolerance, and it consumes the margin from the other side | only with a specification |

The distinction matters because only the second kind can be reduced by gathering data. A model uncertainty needs a better model; an interpretation uncertainty needs a decision; a construction tolerance needs a detail.

## 5. What this changes

**The thinnest positive margin in the project is not demonstrated.** Row 2 reads **+40 mm** of door free width and the declaration calls it the thinnest positive. It is 40 mm because a 900 mm leaf is 40 mm wider than the 860 mm requirement — but the requirement is on the **free** width, and the free width is the leaf minus the frame, hinge and stop build-up. Nothing in the repository says what that build-up is. Ordinary timber door sets spend 30–60 mm on it. So the margin is somewhere between +10 mm and −20 mm, and the sign is unknown.

This does not mean the door fails. It means **the one margin the declaration called positive and thin is not yet a margin at all** — it is a nominal leaf size. The fix is a door schedule with the free width dimensioned, which is a line on a drawing, not a calculation.

**Two rows are zero by construction, and the declaration only admits one.** The transport envelope is flagged as «zero by construction» because every R2 option was solved *to* 4000 mm. The wall U is in the same position and is not: 189 mm is the thickness that makes 0.2202 against a limit of 0.2200, so the margin is zero because the design was solved to the limit, not because the wall happens to land there. **A dimension solved to a limit has no margin by definition**, and the declaration should name both rows as the same failure mode rather than one as a fact and the other as a sign flip.

**Row 13's sign was inverted, not merely uncertain.** The declaration carried it as a thin positive: +0.051 m²K/W with the break built. [The 2D calculation](verificacion-puente-2d.md) shows that the 1D requirement of 0.358 m²K/W was computed on the steel, and the steel **stops governing at the first millimetre of pad** — the timber corner takes over and needs **0.80 m²K/W**. So 9 mm of foam, which the 1D screen priced as sufficient, gives f_Rsi 0.693 against a criterion of 0.754, and the row is negative as drawn. This is the one row where the model uncertainty did not make the margin smaller; it made it the wrong sign, which is why a one-sided bound is not a substitute for a second dimension.

**Row 5 survives, but the published number is mid-band.** Every value from 8 % to 23 % is negative, so the timber fraction cannot rescue the section — it is a lever on the *size* of the deficit, not on its sign. But −422 mm is the 15 % value, and at 23 % the deficit is **−498 mm**. The R2 report's sensitivity table runs down to 8 % and never up, so the project has been quoting a mid-band figure in the position where a worst case belongs. The sign is robust; the magnitude is understated by up to 76 mm.

## 6. What this does not settle

1. **Row 8's model uncertainty is still unquantified.** One junction has now been calculated and it brackets the generic 0.10 W/mK the bridge screen assumed — 0.027 wrapped, 0.195 unwrapped — but the mean Ψ″ rests on 61.10 m of inventory of which one junction is solved. A bound there is honest; a number would not be. Row 13 is no longer in this position: it has a 2D answer, and the answer reversed its sign.
2. **Construction tolerance is absent everywhere.** No row carries it, because no detail and no specification exist. It is the fourth kind and it is entirely missing.
3. **Row 11's site uncertainty is not an interval.** Ground level is a survey result, not a distribution.
4. **Rows 1, 9 and 12 stay undefined.** A product, a limit and a mass are missing, and no sweep can substitute for them.
