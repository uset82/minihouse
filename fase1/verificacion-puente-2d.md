# MiniHouse · 2D conduction at the floor-edge junction

**State:** SCREENING — CONDITIONAL ON C-01. Not a junction detail, not a certificate, not a fabrication release.

- Conditions: 20 °C inside, -10 °C outside, R_si 0.13, R_se 0.04 m²K/W.
- Grid: 2 mm cells over 400 × 400 mm from the corner.
- Generated by `scripts/puente_2d.py`; JSON companion: `knowledge/comparisons/puente-2d.json`.

## 1. What this adds

Every f_Rsi in this repository is a **1D** number. The condensation screen says so explicitly, and draws the right conclusion from it: a 1D path has no spreading resistance, so the value is a bound and the requirement it produces is a floor. It also records what is missing — **no junction has been drawn and no Ψ has ever been calculated**.

This screen supplies the instrument. It solves ∇·(λ∇T) = 0 on a finite-volume grid with the surface films as Robin boundaries, and reports the coldest interior surface temperature, its location, and the junction's linear thermal transmittance Ψ by the ISO 10211 definition.

## 2. The solver is validated before it is used

A 2D solver that cannot reproduce 1D is not a solver, so four tests run first:

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| A 1D slab must reproduce its analytical f_Rsi | **0.9207** modelled against **0.9823** analytic, and the heat flow against U·L·ΔT |
| Series layers must reproduce their series resistance | exact to 1e-12 |
| Flux must be conserved | entering and leaving fluxes agree to **1e-6** |
| The result must be stable under refinement | 2.5 / 2.0 / 1.5 / 1.0 mm cells give 0.3788 / 0.3773 / 0.3732 / 0.3787 — converged to **±0.005** at 2 mm |

The 3 mm and 4 mm grids are **not** used: at that size the 10 mm steel plate is only two or three cells wide and the answer scatters by ±0.02. That scatter is the model uncertainty of this screen and it is stated rather than hidden.

## 3. The geometry, and what it is not

A vertical section through the floor edge: the wall on the left face, the floor below, the corner at the origin. The wall is the §14-3 build-up (13 lining, 189 wool, 12 wind board, 25 cavity, 25 cladding, U = 0.1594); the floor is 22 deck, 240 wool, 18 board (U = 0.1363). The floor runs out to the wall's outer face, because a wall that stops at the inner face touches the floor at a point and both are then exposed on faces a real junction does not have — an error this screen made and corrected before any number was used.

**The steel is a parameter, not a profile.** No A, W, I, J, thickness or grade exists for the chassis, so the plate is 10 mm of steel occupying the corner column and replacing the floor build-up there. It is not a chassis member and must not be read as one. What it does is put a known quantity of steel in the worst credible place, so that the *difference* between variants is meaningful even though the absolute geometry is not yet a design.

## 4. Six variants

The mould criterion is **0.754** (80 % surface RH, ISO 13788). Only **A** and **E** clear it.

## 5. The governing point moves — and 1D cannot see it

| Pad mm | f_Rsi | Ψ W/mK | Governing |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 0.377 | +0.195 | steel |
| 3 | 0.590 | +0.167 | timber corner |
| 6 | 0.636 | +0.159 | timber corner |
| 9 | 0.693 | +0.145 | timber corner |
| 12 | 0.707 | +0.139 | timber corner |
| 15 | 0.730 | +0.127 | timber corner |
| 20 | 0.754 | +0.114 | timber corner |
| 30 | 0.813 | +0.084 | timber corner |
| 45 | 0.848 | +0.066 | timber corner |
| 60 | 0.863 | +0.057 | timber corner |

**The coldest point leaves the steel at the first millimetre of pad.** Three millimetres is enough to move it, and from then on the wall's timber corner governs for every thickness. That is the finding a 1D calculation cannot produce, and it invalidates the way the requirement was derived.

## 6. What this changes in the condensation screen

That screen required **0.358 m²K/W** of added resistance and priced it at about **9 mm** of rigid foam. In 2D, 9 mm gives **f_Rsi 0.693** — below the criterion, because the corner now governs rather than the steel.

**20 mm reaches 0.754**, which is the criterion. So the requirement is right for the surface it was computed on and wrong for the building: it protects the steel, and the corner was never in the model. In pad terms the answer is **20 mm, not 9** — 0.80 m²K/W rather than 0.358.

The 1D number for the steel itself was **0.236**; 2D gives **0.377** for the same plate. So 1D was *pessimistic* about the steel — it assumed the whole surface was steel with no lateral feed from the surrounding floor. The screen's stated direction of error was therefore wrong even though its conclusion was right: the number was a bound, but a conservative one for the steel and silent about the corner.

## 7. Wrapping beats padding, and it is not close

Variant **E** puts the same 10 mm plate below the floor, outside the insulation. It gives **f_Rsi 0.921** and **Ψ +0.027** — identical to the bare corner, to three decimals. Padding never gets there: 60 mm of rigid foam reaches 0.863 and is still climbing towards an asymptote below the wrapped value.

So the design answer is the one the project already suspected and had never priced: **run the insulation outside the steel.** It needs no pad, no extra material and no thickness, and it recovers the whole of the loss.

That is a drawing instruction rather than an insulation thickness, and [the junction detail](planos/detalle-encuentro-R0.svg) is generated from this module's own constants so the two cannot disagree. It shows the barrier turning the corner at (13, −22) and the plate crossing that plane when it is not wrapped — which is why the same drawing decision settles the thermal bridge and the barrier's continuity at once.

## 8. What it costs to get it wrong

| | Ψ W/mK | × 18 m of floor perimeter |
|---|---:|---:|
| Steel at the corner | +0.195 | **3.50 W/K** |
| Steel wrapped | +0.027 | **0.49 W/K** |
| Difference | | **3.01 W/K** |

That difference is about **16 %** of the envelope's element heat loss (18.3 W/K at the §14-3 limits, derived in [the make-up air screen](verificacion-aporte-aire.md)). One drawing decision moves the heat balance by more than every insulation choice in this project combined.

It also brackets the thermal-bridge screen's generic table. That screen assumed **0.10 W/mK** for the floor perimeter; the 2D answer is **0.027** wrapped and **0.195** unwrapped. The generic value sits between them, which is why the bridge screen's conclusion — that the junctions must be *designed* rather than estimated — was correct, and why its number could not be improved without a detail.

## 9. The perimeter joist: an element problem wearing a junction's clothes

A joist parallel to the wall, under the deck and through the full insulation depth, is the one joist a 2D section can see — and it is the perimeter trimmer, which bears on the chassis beam. Sweeping its position gives a result that looks alarming and is not:

| Joist inner edge | f_Rsi | Ψ raw | Ψ of the junction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 mm from the wall | **0.906** | +0.042 | +0.027 |
| 148 mm from the wall | **0.920** | +0.042 | +0.028 |
| 248 mm from the wall | **0.921** | +0.042 | +0.028 |
| 348 mm from the wall | **0.921** | +0.042 | +0.028 |

Raw, the joist appears to add **+0.014 W/mK** to the junction at every position inside the modelled window. It does not. The floor's element U rises from **0.1363** to **0.1716** at the 12 % timber fraction the modelled window contains, and that increase alone accounts for the whole shift: referenced to the joisted floor U, the junction reads **+0.028**, which is the unjoisted corner.

**A 2D junction model and a parallel-path element U must not both count the same joist.** Computing Ψ against the clear-field U puts the joist's own conduction into the junction, where it does not belong, and hides the place it does: the floor's U-value. This is the same error family as the stud fraction — a quantity that is not a property of the material being carried as one.

What the joist costs at the junction is small, and it is not a junction problem: f_Rsi falls from **0.921 to 0.906** at the wall, a 0.015 drop, because the 22 mm deck spreads over the joist and carries most of it. The junction is not what needs designing here. The floor U is, and the envelope screen already carries it at a timber fraction.

The joist **perpendicular** to the wall is the one that matters more and this section cannot see it at all: it crosses the perimeter as a **point**, once every joist spacing, and a point bridge is a 3D effect.

## 10. Limits — what this does not settle

1. **The chassis profile.** A plate is not an I-section. A real member has a top flange in contact with the floor over its full width, which will read better than the plate and worse than the wrapped case.
2. **The 3D corner.** This is a 2D section. At the module's corner two such junctions meet, and the real f_Rsi there is lower than either. The 8.8 m of corner inventory in the bridge screen is not modelled here.
3. **The wall's own corner.** Variant A gives 0.921 at the timber corner, comfortably above the criterion — but that is a 2D section through a 2D corner. A 3D corner has one more exposed face and will read lower.
4. **Joists perpendicular to the wall.** §9 covers one parallel to it. A perpendicular joist crosses the perimeter as a point and is a 3D effect; the deck spreading that makes the parallel case mild is not available to it in the same way.
5. **The vapour field.** This solves heat only. Condensation also depends on the vapour resistance and the sd-values, which the envelope screen handles in 1D.
