## 2. The waste-plan duty hangs on one limb

§9-6 triggers on **>300 m² BRA** for a new build, **>100 m²** for alteration or demolition, or **>10 tonnes** of construction and demolition waste. At 18 m² the area limbs are not close. The tonnage limb is the only live question, and it is a **mass** question.

Waste generated by building the cabin, at plausible cabin masses:

| Cabin mass t | Waste 5 % | Waste 10 % | Waste 20 % | Waste 30 % |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.10 t | 0.20 t | 0.40 t | 0.60 t |
| 3 | 0.15 t | 0.30 t | 0.60 t | 0.90 t |
| 4 | 0.20 t | 0.40 t | 0.80 t | 1.20 t |
| 5 | 0.25 t | 0.50 t | 1.00 t | 1.50 t |
| 6 | 0.30 t | 0.60 t | 1.20 t | 1.80 t |
| 8 | 0.40 t | 0.80 t | 1.60 t | 2.40 t |

**No cell in that table crosses 10 tonnes, and no plausible cabin mass could.** The mass needed to cross the limb at a given waste fraction:

| Waste fraction | Cabin mass needed |
|---:|---:|
| 5 % | 200 t |
| 10 % | 100 t |
| 20 % | 50 t |
| 30 % | 33 t |

An 18 m² cabin weighing 33 t at a 30 % waste fraction is not a building, it is a barge. So:

> **The 10-tonne limb cannot be crossed by building the cabin. It can only be crossed by aggregation.**

§9-6(2) requires that a project comprising several buildings, constructions or installations be assessed **as a whole**. A reclaimed-timber supply chain that dismantles a source building adds that demolition waste to the same assessment. That is how limb (d) engages — not through the cabin, but through where its material came from. §9-8 (70 wt % sorting) and §9-9 (final report) inherit exactly the same switch, so all three stand or fall together.

**Consequence for the project definition.** Whether chapter 9's waste duties apply is decided before the first drawing, by the choice of whether the project includes a demolition. That choice is part of C-08 and should be recorded with it.

## 3. §9-2 and the reclaimed-timber documentation gap

§9-2 requires products «uten eller med lavt innhold av helse- eller miljøfarlige stoffer». Its guidance offers a ladder of compliance instruments, and **every rung requires a manufacturer**:

| Rung | Instrument | Available for reclaimed timber? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Svanemerket / EU Ecolabel, ECOproduct, SINTEF Teknisk Godkjenning | **no** — no product to look up |
| 2 | Sikkerhetsdatablad, EPD, CE declaration of conformity | **no** — none exist |
| 3 | Written confirmation from the manufacturer's legally responsible person | **no** — there is no manufacturer |
| 4 | Professional assessment and comparison of hazardous-substance content | **yes** — the only route left |

The **SCIP** duty (produktforskriften §2-32, >**0.1 weight percent** SVHC) falls on *suppliers of articles*; a private party harvesting timber from a demolished building is not one.

This is not a finding that reclaimed timber is non-compliant. It is a finding that **the pre-accepted documentation route is structurally unavailable**, so rung 4 is the whole strategy. For old timber that means answering, element by element, whether the material carries lead paint, CCA or other preservative treatment, PCB-bearing sealant or asbestos-contaminated dust — which is a **testing** question, and should be scoped before the timber is bought.

## 4. The 0.90 m backflow head is a burial depth

§15-6(2) requires the water level in the **lowest water trap** to sit at least **900 mm** above the internal top of the main pipe. §15-8(3) repeats the same figure for stormwater and combined sewers. With ground level as datum:

```
h_ffl − d_trap + d_crown ≥ 900 mm
```

Rearranged, it is a **minimum burial depth**:

```
d_crown ≥ 900 + d_trap − h_ffl
```

A chassis cabin has a low `h_ffl`. The shallowest crown depth that still complies:

| FFL above ground mm | Trap 50 mm | Trap 60 mm | Trap 75 mm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 250 | 700 | 710 | 725 |
| 300 | 650 | 660 | 675 |
| 350 | 600 | 610 | 625 |
| 400 | 550 | 560 | 575 |
| 450 | 500 | 510 | 525 |
| 500 | 450 | 460 | 475 |

Whether that is a problem depends entirely on what the drain connects to:

| Receiving pipe | Crown depth mm | FFL 400 / trap 60 | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| municipal main (frost depth) | 1500 | 1840 | **passes**, +940 mm |
| shallow sealed-tank connection | 400 | 740 | **fails**, -160 mm |

**So the rule is not a general problem — it is a specific one.** A municipal main sits deep for frost reasons and the head is satisfied with a wide margin. A shallow sealed-tank connection is where it fails, and then it demands roughly **560 mm** of crown depth at a 400 mm floor and a 60 mm trap.

That matters because it is not a plumbing detail but an **earthwork** floor. It pulls against three other requirements at once: the light-touch siting premise of a transportable cabin, §7-1(2) (do not create or worsen a hazard by the works) and §9-3 (do not disturb contaminated ground). The only ways out change the drainage **concept** — a raised floor, a pumped tank, or a different receiving pipe — and that decision has not been taken.

## 5. The stove fixes three things at once

For an emergency cabin in Norway a wood stove is close to certain, and choosing one closes a chain rather than a line item:

| Criterion | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Particle emission | **5 g/kg** with catalyst, **10 g/kg** otherwise | §9-10 |
| Flue-gas temperature class | **T400** minimum | §15-1(5)(d), §15-3(2) |
| Chimney crack visibility | **2 free sides** minimum | §15-3(5)(b) |
| Surface temperature | burn risk above **~45 °C** | §15-1(3)(c) |
| Floor protection | non-combustible slab or **B-s1,d0 (In1)** | §15-1(3)(b) |
| Cleaning | access for **feiing** and soot removal | §15-3(5)(d) |

The **two free sides** rule is the one that reaches the floor plan. An element chimney boxed into a wall or a cupboard on three sides does not comply, because a crack could not be seen. On an 18 m² plan that is a positional constraint competing with the stove's own clearances and with the 45 °C surface limit — three constraints on one small area of wall.

## 6. Two frequencies that must not be conflated

§15-8(1) requires stormwater to be dimensioned for **climate-adjusted 100-year** rainfall. §7-2 assigns MiniHouse safety class **F2 = 1/200**. These are different instruments and the ordering is coherent:

- the **drain** is sized for the 1/100 rain (design capacity);
- the **building** must be safe at the 1/200 flood (safety class).

Collapsing them produces either an over-sized drain or an under-protected floor level. Where the two meet is the finished floor level: §7-2 pushes it up and §15-8(3) pushes backflow protection onto the drain. Combined with §15-6's 0.90 m rule pushing the pipe down, the three requirements pull the same geometry in opposite directions, and the space between them is the design space.

## 7. What is not applicable, and why that is a result

| Item | Why not |
|---|---|
| §15-2 central heating | a single stove or panel heaters are not a distribution system |
| §15-9…15-15 lifts and platforms | no lifting device for persons; a one-storey cabin has none |
| Chapter 16 lift safety control | no lift; the chapter is an operational-safety regime, not documentation |
| §17-1 greenhouse gas account | *boligblokk* and *yrkesbygning* only; a småhus is exempt **by definition**, not by area |

The §17-1 exemption is worth one sentence of honesty. A project built on material efficiency and reuse sits outside the only climate-accounting requirement in the regulation, and the NS 3720 method **excludes biogenic carbon** — which is precisely the argument reclaimed timber would want to make. If the account is ever produced voluntarily, that limitation has to be stated with it.
