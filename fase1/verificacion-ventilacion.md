## 1. Why this screen exists

§13-1(1) requires ventilation that secures **satisfactory air quality**, adapted to the room design, the use, the pollution and the moisture load. It gives no number. On the mikrohus route §13-2's numeric rates do **not** apply, and the guidance is explicit that alternative air quantities may be used **but must be documented** ([S023](../knowledge/sources/S023.md)).

The project has recorded that sentence repeatedly and has never produced the quantities. This is the last requirement in the §2-2(2) list classified as an **analysis** that had no analysis behind it.

## 2. The quantities, documented

The most defensible alternative is not an invented number: it is §13-2's own rates, adopted voluntarily, because they are the regulation's statement of what satisfactory means for a dwelling. **They do not bind here** — that distinction is the whole reason the sentence has to be written down rather than assumed.

| Quantity | Value | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Whole dwelling | **21.6 m³/h** | 18 m² × 1.2 m³/h·m² (§13-2) |
| Bed places | **52.0 m³/h** | 2 × 26 m³/h |
| Governing | **52.0 m³/h** | the larger of the two |
| Extract, basic | **90.0 m³/h** | kitchen 36 + bathroom 54 |
| Extract, boosted — one room | **108.0 m³/h** | the larger single figure |
| Extract, boosted — both rooms | **216.0 m³/h** | kitchen 108 + bathroom 108 |

The fitted extract is the kitchen and the bathroom, and those two dominate the requirement: the pre-accepted volumes are **larger than the whole-dwelling rate**, which is normal and is why the extract governs the design rather than the supply figure.

**The source does not say whether both wet rooms boost simultaneously**, so both readings are carried rather than one being chosen. The second is the upper bound, and it is the one that exposes the problem below.

## 3. The problem the rates hide: a fan in a sealed box does not move air

A mechanical extract lowers the pressure until the envelope's leakage supplies the difference. With `Q = Q50 · (Δp/50)^n` and `Q50 = 1.5 × 36.9 = 55.4 m³/h`, the cabin settles at:

| Extract mode | Extract m³/h | Room pressure Pa | Achievable at ≤150 Pa? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| basic | 90 | **106** | yes, at the top end |
| boosted, one room | 108 | **140** | yes, at the top end |
| boosted, both rooms | 216 | **406** | **no — the fan stalls** |

A domestic extract fan develops of the order of 50–150 Pa, and at that top end it can pull only **113 m³/h** through this envelope. So the last row is not a pressure the cabin would sit at — it is a flow the fan **cannot deliver**. The specified boosted rate is unachievable without make-up air, and the shortfall is a ventilation deficit, not a fan selection problem.

**Even the deliverable rows are not small numbers.** A chimney's natural draught is of the order of 10–20 Pa, so at **106 Pa** of negative pressure the flue does not merely underperform — it reverses. And §13-5 applies to this building: sustained negative pressure is exactly the mechanism that draws soil gas through the floor and defeats a radon barrier, whatever its airtightness.

So the design is not a fan schedule. It is a decision about **where the make-up air comes from**, and every answer is a hole in the vapour barrier.

[The make-up air screen](verificacion-aporte-aire.md) sizes that decision, and tests whether the same opening can rescue the flue. It cannot — which is why this screen ends on a decision and the next one ends on a specification.

## 4. The stove, and why it cannot be an afterthought

The margin against the flue reversing is **−130 Pa** at the highest pressure the fan can actually hold (140 Pa, boosted, one room) — negative, which is the finding. A wood stove in this cabin needs **its own outdoor air intake**, not a share of the room air, and that intake is a fourth penetration of the barrier.

## 5. The penetrations, counted

The envelope's moisture strategy rests on a continuous warm-side barrier at Sd ≥ 10 m. Each of these interrupts it, and none of them is optional:

| Penetration | What it must do |
|---|---|
| cooker-hood extract | §11-10: own duct, grease filter, EI 15 A2-s1,d0 if not in a shaft |
| bathroom extract | wet-room extract, ducted outside the barrier |
| make-up air intake | the one that decides the pressure balance |
| stove combustion air | direct from outside, below the firebox |
| stove flue | through the roof; the barrier is interrupted at the ceiling |
| pressure-equalising vent | only if the design chooses a passive route |

The [2D junction screen](verificacion-puente-2d.md) says in as many words that it cannot see the barrier penetration: convective leakage through a barrier defect carries far more vapour than diffusion and is invisible to any conduction model. This screen does not model it either — it **counts** it, which is the most that can be done before a detail exists.

## 6. The loop this does not close

The [condensation screen](verificacion-condensacion-union.md) assumes **50 % indoor RH**, and its requirement scales hard with it — 40→60 % multiplies the required break by about 3.5×. Ventilation is what sets that RH, so the two are one problem.

This screen does **not** close it, deliberately. A steady-state mass balance without the timber buffering gives an indoor concentration above saturation at any plausible rate, which is obviously wrong — the buffering is not a correction, it is most of the answer in a cabin whose interior is 48 m² of timber. A defensible closure needs a dynamic sorption model and material data the project does not hold.

What can be said is the **direction**: the 50 % assumption is conservative, because lower RH reduces the required break. If the ventilation design holds the cabin below 50 %, the junction requirement only gets easier. That is worth stating precisely because the opposite would be a design emergency.

## 7. Limits

- The rates in §2 are §13-2's, **adopted voluntarily**. They do not bind on this route and must not be reported as a requirement.
- The leakage exponent 0.65 is a generic value. The pressures scale with it, and a measured n would move them.
- The stove's combustion air and the flue draught are **assumption ranges**, not source values. The comparison is the finding; the exact figures are not.
- No duct is sized, no fan is selected and no make-up air path is designed. This is a pressure screen, not a ventilation design.
- §13-5's radon limit is not assessed. The screen only states that the mechanism is engaged.
- Nothing here is a compliance statement or a design release.
