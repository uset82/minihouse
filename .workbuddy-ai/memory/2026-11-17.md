## Envelope thermal and moisture screening (§14-3 / §13-13)

- **Two modelling rules decide the answer, and both cut against optimism.** (1) ISO 6946: for a *well-ventilated* cavity, ignore the cavity and everything outside it and take R_se at the face bounding it. My first pass counted cavity + cladding as insulation, giving U 0.194 for the wall; the correct value is **0.209**. That 0.02 W/m²K is enough to flip a verdict. (2) §14-3's U is a **whole-element** value, so the stud parallel path must be in it.
- Result: clear-field U wall 0.209 / roof 0.164 / floor 0.160 — **all three pass** — and at a **15 % stud fraction** 0.267 / 0.218 / 0.209 — **all three fail**. Penalty 20–30 %. Insulation needed: wall ~**189 mm** (141 clear), roof ~247, floor ~238. This is the third constraint converging on the same space: it competes with the 2.2 m room height and the 4.00 m transport limit.
- Moisture inverted my expectation. Steady-state Glaser with a **vapour-open wood-fibre wind barrier** stays **dry at 40 % and 55 % RH whether the barrier is Sd 10 m, Sd 2 m or absent** — nothing traps the vapour. What fails is the opposite: **no barrier + tight sheathing (Sd 2 m)** condenses **0.41 kg/m² in 90 d at 40 % RH, 1.09 at 55 %**. So the dominant failure mode is a **cold-side trap**, not a missing warm-side barrier.

### Chapter 9 — a structural correction, not just a retrieval

Chapter 9 is routinely assumed to be the "noise, water and sewage" chapter. It is not.
Noise is §13-6 (exempt on this route), water and sewage are chapter 15. Chapter 9 is
pollution, hazardous substances, ground, nature types and **construction waste**. Carded
as S027 with a full applicability map.

Three parts bite:

- **§9-2 and reclaimed timber.** The provision asks for products with no or low
  hazardous-substance content, which reclaimed timber may satisfy. What it cannot do is
  *demonstrate* it: every rung of the guidance's compliance ladder (Svanemerket, EU
  Ecolabel, ECOproduct, SINTEF TG, safety data sheet, EPD, CE declaration of conformity,
  manufacturer's written confirmation) **presupposes a manufacturer**, and reclaimed
  timber has none. SCIP (>0.1 wt% SVHC) falls on *suppliers of articles*, which a private
  party harvesting from a demolition is not. Only the "professional assessment and
  comparison" route remains — a **testing** question (lead paint, CCA, PCB sealant,
  asbestos dust), with a cost and a lead time. This reframes C-08 from a sourcing question
  into a testing question, and the test should be scoped before the timber is bought.
- **§9-3 is a second siting gate**, independent of chapter 7: chapter 7 asks whether nature
  threatens the building, §9-3 whether the ground threatens the occupants. Municipal
  tiltaksplan must be approved before any excavation; the municipality may prohibit.
- **§9-5 is the chapter's gift** — design for later disassembly, documented in the
  prosjektering, with **fastening as the decisive detail**. It rewards the concept, but
  warns that a welded chassis-to-frame or a bonded membrane is "difficult to separate" and
  therefore poorly suited to reuse.

The waste duties turned out sharper than expected. §9-6 triggers on >300 m² (new build),
>100 m² (alteration/demolition) or >10 t waste. At 18 m² the area limbs are unreachable,
and the mass sweep shows the cabin **cannot** cross the tonnage limb at any plausible mass:
33 t needed at a 30 % waste fraction, 200 t at 5 %. So:

> **The 10-tonne limb can only be crossed by §9-6(2) aggregation with a demolition.**

Which means §9-6/§9-8/§9-9 apply **iff the project includes dismantling a source building**
— a decision that belongs to C-08 and is made before the first drawing. §9-7 covers
existing buildings only, so a new-build cabin is outside it, but the source building is not.

### Chapters 15/16/17 — mostly negative space, made explicit

**Chapter 15 has no small-building exemption anywhere.** Checked §15-5, §15-6, §15-7,
§15-8 one by one; each states none. So the cabin is fully in scope for §15-1 (45 °C burn
risk, B-s1,d0 floor protection, T400), §15-3 (T400, constant section, **≥2 free sides**,
feiing access), §15-5 (38/55 °C, 20/50 mm air gaps, leak stopper, stoppekran) and §15-6
(water trap, 0.90 m head).

**The 0.90 m rule is a burial depth, not a plumbing detail.** Rearranged:
`d_crown ≥ 900 + d_trap − h_ffl`. At a 400 mm floor and 60 mm trap that is ~560 mm of
cover. A municipal main clears it by ~940 mm; a shallow sealed-tank connection **fails by
160 mm**. The route out changes the drainage *concept* (raised floor, pumped tank), not a
fitting, and it pulls against the light-touch siting premise, §7-1(2) and §9-3 at once.

Also caught: §15-8(1)'s **100-year** stormwater figure is a *design capacity* and §7-2's
**1/200** is a *safety class* — different instruments, and collapsing them gives either an
oversized drain or an under-protected floor level.

**Four determinations** (not omissions): §15-2 central heating (no distribution system),
§§15-9…15-15 lifting devices for persons (none), **chapter 16 = lift safety control, not
documentation** (a wrong assumption I held until I fetched the chapter index), and §17-1
greenhouse gas account — *boligblokk* and *yrkesbygning* only, a småhus exempt **by
definition**, not by area. The irony is worth recording: a reuse project sits outside the
only climate-accounting requirement, and NS 3720 excludes biogenic carbon, which is exactly
the argument reclaimed timber would want to make.

## Condensación en el encuentro del chasis (check 23) — el hueco entre §14-3 y §13-13

## Adecuación de márgenes: ninguna fila pasa el test que la propia declaración fija (check 26)

- Ψ″: −0,067 (buen detalle) → −0,347 (malo). Banda **0,28**, todo negativo.

**Los tres hallazgos que importan:**
1. **El +40 mm de la puerta no es un margen, es una hoja nominal.** El requisito es sobre el ancho *libre* = hoja − bastidor − bisagra − tope, y nada en el repo dice cuánto es. Un juego corriente se lleva 30–60 mm → el margen está entre +10 y −20 mm y **el signo es desconocido**. Era «el margen positivo más fino del proyecto» y no está demostrado.

## El rastrel perimetral: vía paralela, no puente de unión

Añadido al solver un rastrel de 48 mm paralelo a la pared, bajo el entablado y atravesando todo el aislamiento — el único que una sección 2D puede ver, y que es el vilo perimetral que apoya en la viga del chasis.

En bruto parece sumar **+0,014 W/mK** al encuentro en toda posición dentro de la ventana. No lo hace: la U del suelo sube de **0,1363** a **0,1716** con la fracción del 12 % que contiene la ventana modelada, y eso explica el desplazamiento entero. Referido a la U del suelo **con** rastrel, el encuentro lee **+0,028** — la esquina sin rastrel.

**Regla:** un modelo 2D de unión y una U de elemento por vía paralela **no pueden contar el mismo rastrel dos veces**. Es la misma familia de error que la fracción de montantes. Lo que el rastrel cuesta en la unión es poco (f_Rsi 0,921 → 0,906) porque el entablado de 22 mm reparte; lo que cuesta de verdad está en la U del suelo, que la pantalla de envolvente ya lleva con fracción de madera. El rastrel **perpendicular** cruza el perímetro como punto y es 3D.

Dos defectos propios más, ambos de forma y ambos vistos al releer el render: la sección nueva usaba `L` cuando el acumulador se llama `lines`, y el informe acabó con **dos secciones «10»**.

## Las ventanas y la puerta son una sola pregunta (comprobación 28)
