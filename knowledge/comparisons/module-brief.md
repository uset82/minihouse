## 2. Cold-climate envelope — governing principles

From S010, apply the four-condition moisture test (moisture, path, force, susceptible material) to each of these:

| Failure mechanism | Route/force | Control in this module |
|---|---|---|
| Rain penetration through reclaimed timber cladding | Absorption, penetration, splash | Vented rainscreen cavity, continuous top-to-bottom, with vent openings top and bottom; overlapping/flashed joints; drained cavity |
| Interstitial condensation at steel studs (thermal bridging) | Vapour diffusion + convection through air leaks | Continuous air barrier; correctly placed vapour control layer on the warm side; thermal break at steel-to-envelope interfaces; avoid concealed steel cold spots |
| Built-in moisture in reclaimed timber | Water in timber at installation | Measure moisture content before enclosure; allow drying; do not close wet timber behind vapour-impermeable layers |
| Ground capillary rise at chassis/floor | Wicking from support | Capillary break between chassis and ground/support; drainage away from base; keep cladding clear of standing water |
| Wind-buffeting rain during transport | Wind-driven rain while wrapped | Weather-tight temporary wrap during transport (S008 §7.1), with joints detailed against ingress |

Ventilation drying (S010) is especially important where vapour-impermeable claddings or roofing are used — provide clear air spaces and vent holes.

### The envelope now has numbers

[Envelope screening](../../fase1/verificacion-envolvente.md) turns the principles above into element-level values, with generic material data and no products selected.

| Element | Clear-field U | U at 15 % studs | §14-3 limit | Insulation for the limit (clear / 15 % studs) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Wall | 0.209 | **0.267** | 0.22 | 141 / **189** mm |
| Roof | 0.164 | **0.218** | 0.18 | 181 / **247** mm |
| Floor | 0.160 | **0.209** | 0.18 | 176 / **238** mm |

**Every element passes on the clear field and fails once the studs are counted.** The penalty is 20–30 % for a timber fraction of 10–20 % of the insulated area, and it is invisible in a layer-by-layer calculation. Continuous insulation, a cross-braced service zone or an insulating sheathing board is therefore not a refinement — it is what decides whether §14-3 is met.

Two modelling rules do most of the work, and both cut against optimism. First, **a well-ventilated cavity and everything outside it are excluded from the U-value** (ISO 6946), so the reclaimed cladding contributes nothing thermally — counting it flatters the wall by about 0.02 W/m²K, enough to turn a fail into a pass. Second, **§14-3's value is a whole-element value**, so the parallel path through the studs has to be in it.

The moisture result is the more interesting one, and it is not the usual rule of thumb. A steady-state Glaser profile of the same wall shows that **with a vapour-open wood-fibre wind barrier the wall stays dry at 40 % and even at 55 % indoor RH regardless of whether the barrier is Sd 10 m, Sd 2 m or absent** — nothing traps the vapour. What fails is the opposite mistake: **no barrier plus a tight sheathing board (Sd 2 m)** condenses **0.41 kg/m² in 90 days at 40 % RH and 1.09 kg/m² at 55 %**. The dominant failure mode in this build-up is a **cold-side trap**, not a missing warm-side barrier.

That does **not** make §13-13's Sd ≥ 10 m redundant. Glaser sees one of four moisture paths; it cannot see air leakage, which carries far more vapour than diffusion and is invisible to a one-dimensional model. The Sd ≥ 10 m requirement is best read as a **robustness requirement** — it keeps the wall dry when the cold side is not as open as designed, when indoor humidity runs high, when the membrane is also doing air-barrier duty, and when the detail is built by someone who has not read the analysis. The regulation is buying tolerance, not a number.

**The junctions are the part of the envelope that no requirement is watching.** §14-3 caps the element U-values and the airtightness and says nothing about linear bridges; the only figure in the retrieved regulation, ≤**0.05 W/m²K** normalised, sits in §14-2's route, which §14-5(1) exempts at this size. An 18 m² plan carries **61.10 m** of linear detail — 3.39 m per m² of floor, because the floor perimeter alone is as long as the building. At ordinary detailing that reads **0.210 W/m²K**, 4.2× the benchmark and about **16 %** of total heat loss; even good detailing is 2.3×. Insulating it away is arithmetically possible and geometrically shut: +53 mm of wall and +65 mm of roof, and the roof share alone costs **69 mm of eave height** against a 422 mm deficit. So the junctions have to be **designed**, and the chassis profile and the thermal detail are one drawing. One caution on the benchmark itself — it is normalised by floor area, and at 18 m² it implies a mean Ψ of 0.0147 W/mK, under half of good practice, so it should not be adopted as a project target. State bridge targets **per metre**, where the number means the same thing at any size. See [puentes térmicos](../../fase1/verificacion-puentes-termicas.md).

**And the junction is a moisture problem before it is an energy problem.** §14-3 caps heat and §13-13 caps vapour, but neither says anything about the temperature of a steel member crossing the insulation line — which is what decides whether the junction condenses. A bare member gives a 1D temperature factor of **0.236** where the mould criterion needs **0.754**, so its surface is wet and frozen for most of a Bergen heating season, and a junction that wet will rot the timber it touches whatever the rest of the wall does.

**The 2D calculation has now been done and it changed the answer twice.** Solved properly ([2D junction](../../fase1/verificacion-puente-2d.md), ISO 10211), the same steel plate reads **0.377**, not 0.236 — lateral feed from the surrounding floor warms it, so the 1D number was pessimistic about the steel. But the steel is the coldest point **only while it is bare**: from the first millimetre of pad the **timber wall corner** takes over and stays governing at every thickness. So the requirement is **0.80 m²K/W — 20 mm of rigid foam, not 9** — and 9 mm gives 0.693 against 0.754. Size for mould, not for condensation.

**The better fix is not a thickness at all.** Putting the steel **below the floor, outside the insulation**, restores **0.921** and **Ψ +0.027** — identical to the bare timber corner, with no pad and no extra material. Padding 60 mm reaches only 0.863. Over 18 m of perimeter the difference between the two positions is **3.01 W/K**, about **16 %** of the envelope's element heat loss. One drawing decision, worth more than every insulation choice in this project combined. See [condensación del encuentro](../../fase1/verificacion-condensacion-union.md).

**And the ventilation that keeps that junction dry is what puts the stove at risk.** §13-1(1) asks for documented alternative air quantities and the project had never produced them; [the ventilation screen](../../fase1/verificacion-ventilacion.md) does, at **52 m³/h** governing supply against a **90 m³/h** basic and **108–216 m³/h** boosted extract. The rates are not the finding. The pressure is: a fan in a sealed 36.9 m³ cabin only moves what the envelope leaks, so at the §14-3 airtightness the extract holds the cabin at **106–140 Pa** negative — against a **10–20 Pa** chimney draught. The flue reverses by **−130 Pa**, and the both-boosted flow needs 406 Pa, which no fan develops. **Rows 13 and 14 of the declaration are the same drawing**: the make-up air has to enter somewhere, and every entry is a hole in the barrier the moisture strategy depends on. **That hole now has a size, and it does not buy the flue.** [The make-up air screen](../../fase1/verificacion-aporte-aire.md) sizes it: a **113 mm** inlet at **10 Pa** of design depression removes **98 Pa**, so the ventilation and the §13-5 driving force are solved by one wall valve. The flue is not: the only depression that passes the §2-2(3) adequacy test needs a **181 mm** inlet at 2 Pa, and 2 Pa is below the pressure a 3 m/s breeze puts on a wall — **a passive inlet cannot hold a set pressure**. So row 14 is not improved by a hole, it is **retired by a room-sealed stove**, which is a specification requirement with no dimensional cost. Two consequences worth carrying: the make-up air costs **905 W** at 30 K, **1.65×** the fabric loss, so heat recovery is a precondition; and by diffusion the hole is **0.8 %** of the barrier's conductance, so the risk is convective and the inlet is a **control problem**.

**The roof can be frozen now, and that is not what the card said.** S024 records that whether the roof belongs to the main load-bearing system must be settled before the build-up is frozen. It need not be: all four branches of §11-4(3) — no. 4 (R 15) and no. 6.1, 6.2, 6.3 — demand **A2-s1,d0 insulation**, because §11-9 D's main rule applies on its own and no. 7's escape route requires a load-bearing substrate meeting A2-s1,d0, which a timber rafter is not. So the insulation is **mineral wool** under every reading, and **no. 6.2 is closed** outright because it describes a wholly non-combustible roof. What stays open is the **rafter** and the **lining**. If R 15 applies, it is a charring problem: at 15 minutes the effective loss is 15.0 mm per face, a rafter exposed on three sides loses it twice across its width, and **48 × 198 keeps only 32 % of its section modulus** — parity needs 78 mm, so 98 mm stock. The way out is a protective lining, which is what no. 6.3 wants anyway: three clauses, one drawing. See [determinación de la cubierta](../../fase1/determinacion-cubierta-incendio.md).

## 3. Reclaimed timber

- Default role: **reversible cladding/finish**, not structural (rules.md §2, SN-CEN/TS 1090-201 for steel reuse; NS-EN 1995 if timber ever carries load).
- Per-piece/lot record: species if known, origin, moisture content, defects, previous use, proposed location. Traceability before installation.
- Any structural use needs documented species, grade, moisture, defects and connections — otherwise stay out of the structural model.

**Room height is the live constraint, and it is worse than the first screen suggested.** §12-7(2)(d) lets parts of a room be lower and permits a sloped ceiling below 2.2 m, so the eave is not the room-height datum — but the roof is not a line either. A real roof build-up costs **t/cos θ** vertically, and the envelope screen pushes the floor from an assumed 150 mm to about **250 mm**. [Verificación de sección](../../fase1/verificacion-seccion.md) solves floor, wall, roof, pitch and stack together, and the answer is blunt: **at 20° with a 1100 mm stack, a 250 mm floor and a 300 mm roof, the wall cannot even reach 2.2 m** — the tallest possible eave is **1785 mm**, and only a **~720 mm band** along the ridge qualifies. The steepest pitch that gives 2.2 m over the full width ranges from **16.3°** (900 mm stack, 200 mm floor, 250 mm roof) down to **1.9°** (1100 / 300 / 350).

Two of the five levers are **closed**: the floor and roof build-ups would have to go **negative** to restore full coverage at the reference case, and they are already at the §14-3 minimum. What is left is a lower trailer stack, a transport dispensation, or a shallower pitch. That is a real design decision, not a detail, and it needs C-02 and C-06 together.

[Opciones R2](../../fase1/opciones-seccion-R2.md) turns that conflict into four configurations, each solved against the same section model: **R2-A** shallow gable at 8° with full headroom, **R2-B** the 20° gable with low side zones, **R2-C** 15° on a 700 mm low-loader deck, **R2-D** 20° at a 4.40 m dispensation. Two findings matter more than the table. First, **R2-B's low zones are not waste**: at 20° each long wall carries a strip **1141 mm deep and 6.24 m² in plan**, with the ceiling running from 1785 mm up to 2.2 m — enough for a bathroom, a 600 mm kitchen run and full-height storage, which is the ordinary logic of an attic flat. Second, **no option has any tolerance**: every one solves exactly to the limit, and a **100 mm** reserve for ridge caps, overhangs or lifting ears takes R2-B from 24 % coverage to **6 %** and R2-D from 97 % to 79 %, while R2-A and R2-C survive intact. R2-B is therefore a contingent option, not a robust one. No option gives a sleeping loft: the ridge free height is 2331 mm, so there is no 1600 mm of headroom above a 2.2 m loft floor. Two corrections have since been applied to that table. The build-ups it was solved with — floor 250, roof 300 — are round numbers, not the envelope solver's output, which returns **281 / 278 mm** at a 15 % timber fraction; the two errors nearly cancel, so the published wall heights are only about **7 mm** optimistic. And **R2-D's 4.40 m dispensation is 22 mm short**: full coverage at 20° needs **4415 mm** even at the published build-ups and **4422 mm** at 15 %, so 4400 mm was never the threshold — it was a round number that landed at 97 %. The timber fraction is the one lever that moves this: at 8 % the dispensation needs only **4358 mm** and R2-B reaches 34 % coverage. Since the number written in the application is the number that gets assessed, that matters before the application is drafted, not after.

**Two door and stair facts that contradict intuition.** The **0.86 m door width does not come from §12-13(2)(a)** — that paragraph is exempt. It comes from **§11-14** (escape route free width, risk class 4). And there is **no 2.0 m door height** requirement from chapter 12, because §12-13(2)(e) is exempt; §11-14 asks only for «tilstrekkelig … høyde». A stair to a sleeping loft is governed by handrails, uniform rise and 0.25 m going — **not** by a width or headroom figure — and §12-14(5)(b) can exempt an operation-only stair, **but not if it is part of the escape route**.

**Three fire findings that change the design, not just the paperwork.** First, the **roof build-up is constrained**: under §11-4(3) no. 6 the roof may go without specified fire resistance only if it is not decisive for stability in the escape phase, and then via 6.1 (two-sided separating element), 6.2 (all roof materials **including insulation** A2-s1,d0) or 6.3 (K210 B-s1,d0 below, **K210 D-s2,d0 permitted in risk class 4**, insulation A2-s1,d0). Criterion 6.2 is incompatible with a timber roof and wood-fibre insulation; 6.3 forces mineral wool, and §11-9 D no. 7 independently requires an **A2-s1,d0 load-bearing substrate** that a timber rafter does not provide. Second, the **escape-window geometry fails**: of the current openings only **V-01** (1200 × 1100, sill 900) satisfies §11-13(4); **V-02** has a 1100 mm sill against a 1000 mm limit, and **V-03** fails height (500 < 600), sum (1000 < 1500) and sill (1500 > 1000). Either the aperture table changes or the single-direction route needs an explicit justification. Third, **the timber interior survives, but conditionally**: §11-9 Table 1A only asks **D-s2,d0** on walls and ceiling for a fire cell up to 200 m², which timber normally achieves, and the external cladding only **D-s3,d0** with unclassified cavity surfaces allowed in BKL 1. That holds **only if the cabin is not treated as an escape route** — if it is, interior surfaces rise to **B-s1,d0**, which is materially harder for reclaimed timber. The kitchen extract must also run in its own **A2-s1,d0** duct with a grease filter and **EI 15 A2-s1,d0** if not in a shaft (§11-10).

**Chapter 7 is the only chapter that can eliminate a site on its own.** Its four sections carry almost no numbers that reach a detail drawing, and that is misleading: §7-2 and §7-3 decide *where* the cabin may stand, and for a Bergen plot — steep terrain, heavy rainfall, marine-limit clay, long coastline — that question arrives before any envelope decision. The classes are settled (**F2 1/200** flood, **S2 1/1000** landslide) and, like the fire class, they do not wait on C-01. What is *not* settled is the plot: no hazard map, flood level or marine-limit check has been done. Two consequences are worth carrying into the site search. First, **a light building is not a protected building** — where a flood area would have great depth or strong current, the guidance moves the target to **F3 (1/1000)**, and a cabin floats or is pushed before it is submerged. Second, **quick clay is not screened by a slope calculation**: below the marine limit the ground must be investigated even outside mapped zones, under NVE 1/2019, and the tiltakskategori cannot be assumed to be K0 for a new dwelling. The one physical number that does reach the design is the **~50 kPa** ceiling for designing *against* a landslide — about 5.1 t/m² — which is a small-event load, not a debris flow carrying boulders.

The envelope can now be specified numerically at the TEK17 minimum level for the detached-building route, but final U-values and any heat-loss/thermal-bridge documentation still depend on use classification; a full §14-2 energy-frame calculation is not triggered by the likely routes.

**§9-2 is where the reclaimed-timber strategy meets its hardest constraint, and it is a paperwork problem rather than a material one.** The provision asks for products «uten eller med lavt innhold av helse- eller miljøfarlige stoffer», which reclaimed timber may well satisfy. What it cannot do is *demonstrate* it: the guidance's ladder of instruments — Svanemerket, EU Ecolabel, ECOproduct, SINTEF Teknisk Godkjenning, safety data sheet, EPD, CE declaration of conformity, or a written confirmation from the manufacturer's legally responsible person — every rung assumes a manufacturer exists. Reclaimed timber has none, and the SCIP notification duty at **>0.1 weight percent** SVHC falls on *suppliers of articles*, which a private party harvesting from a demolition is not. So the only route left is the one the guidance also names: a **professional assessment and comparison** of hazardous-substance content. For old timber that means answering, element by element, whether it carries lead paint, CCA or another preservative, PCB-bearing sealant or asbestos-contaminated dust. That is a **testing** question with a cost and a lead time, and it belongs at the front of C-08 rather than at the end of it.

**The installations chapter hides one geometric constraint that reaches the site plan.** §15-6(2) and §15-8(3) both require the water level in the lowest trap to sit **0.90 m** above the internal top of the main pipe, which rearranges to `d_crown ≥ 900 + d_trap − h_ffl`. A cabin on a chassis has a low `h_ffl`, so at a 400 mm floor and a 60 mm trap the drain needs a **minimum burial depth** of about **560 mm**. Whether that is a problem depends entirely on what the drain connects to: a municipal main sits deep for frost reasons and clears the rule by roughly **940 mm**, while a shallow sealed-tank connection fails it by **160 mm**. The consequence is that the drainage **concept** — mains, gravity tank or pumped tank — is a siting decision with earthwork attached, and earthwork is what a light-touch transportable cabin is trying not to do. It also pulls against §7-1(2) and §9-3 at the same time. A second trap is worth naming: §15-8(1)'s **climate-adjusted 100-year** stormwater figure is a *design capacity*, and §7-2's **1/200** is a *safety class*. They are different instruments, and collapsing them produces either an over-sized drain or an under-protected floor level.

**Chapter 2 is the chapter that judges the method, and it makes one distinction the project has been blurring.** §2-2(1) requires the performance requirements **given** in the regulation to be met; §2-2(2) allows a **functional** requirement to be documented by pre-accepted performance or by **analysis**. Read together that is a gate rather than a permission: **analysis is available only where no number was given.** You can analyse your way *up to* a functional requirement; you cannot analyse your way *past* a stated performance. So the §14-3 U-values, R 15, the 2.2 m room height, the 0.86 m door width, the 0.90 m backflow head, the 10-tonne waste limb, F2/S2 and the 100-year stormwater basis are all simply to be met. What is analysable is the §12-7(2)(d) coverage fraction and the §13-1(1) air quality — and what is neither is the fire question of whether the roof is main-system or whether one escape window suffices, because those ask **which** ytelse applies. That is a classification, and a determination answers it where an analysis cannot.

**§2-2(3) then sets the standard for the analysis, and the project meets three of its four demands.** Method shown suitable and valid, assumptions described, assumptions justified — all present across the thirteen screening reports. The fourth, **necessary safety margins stated**, is met by only three, and the three use different currencies: the **4.00 m** transport height is a screening value from S022 and explicitly not a route permit; the **100 mm** in the R2 options is a project reserve; the **1.4** in the natural-hazard screen is a material factor flagged unverified because the NA is paywalled. §2-2(3) asks for one coherent statement of what margin is being relied on and why it is enough. **That statement now exists** ([declaración de márgenes](../../fase1/declaracion-de-margenes.md)) and it fixes one currency per analysis type; its answer is that the project is short of margin rather than of a statement. The **Limits** sections that every report already carries stopped being good manners at that point and became regulatory work.

The roof branch no longer blocks and the wood-fibre question is settled by it.

**The escape windows and the door width are one question, and the project had been pricing them as two.** §11-13(4) is written as an **alternative** — the exit leads *either* to a safe place, *or* to a rømningsvei with a single direction and then the windows are required — and §11-14 governs that rømningsvei and the doors in or to it. So if the door discharges to a safe place, (4) is not engaged and there is no rømningsvei for §11-14 to reach: **declaration rows 2, 3 and 4 all stop being requirements at once**, and the tally falls from 2 positive / 6 negative to 1 positive / 4 negative over ten rows. If the exit leads to a rømningsvei, all three are mandatory and buildable for **0.29 m²** of glass and a 100 mm sill. **What is not coupled is §11-9:** a fire cell that contains no rømningsvei is still a fire cell, so **D-s2,d0** governs walls and ceiling whatever §11-13 decides — and that is the answer that keeps reclaimed timber exposed. Coupling the two would have condemned the interior on a reading that does not reach it. Travel inside the cabin is **5.04 m** against the 15 m figure the source contains, but the applicable Table 1 row is still missing. See [determinación de la estrategia](../../fase1/determinacion-estrategia-incendio.md).
- **Envelope blocker:** the wall needs ~189 mm of mineral wool at a 15 % stud fraction to meet §14-3 once thermal bridging counts, and the roof ~247 mm. Those thicknesses compete directly with the **2.2 m** room height and the **4.00 m** transport limit, so the build-up is not a free parameter. The chassis-to-wall junction is a linear thermal bridge that **§14-3 does not limit** and that has now been screened rather than solved — 61.10 m of detail, 0.210 W/m²K at ordinary detailing, and no Ψ calculated because no junction is drawn. The material properties used are generic rather than manufacturer data.
**The windows are the cheapest thing in the project to get right, and the cheapest to get wrong.** Chapter 11 asks for an exit to a safe place, and the cabin's door discharges straight to grade — so on the narrowest reading it needs no escape windows at all. On the widest it needs one in every room without a direct exit, and the bathroom has none that qualifies. The gap between those two readings is a **100 mm** sill drop on the kitchen window and **0.29 m²** on the bathroom window, and the bathroom's own back wall is too short to take it, so the window moves to the gable. Designing to the widest reading costs a tenth of a square metre and removes the question; the same change also drops the daylight requirement by 0.06 of light transmittance, which is the only place in the project where one decision fixes two screens. See [ventanas](../../fase1/verificacion-ventanas.md).

- **Margin blocker (§2-2(3)):** the single declaration now exists ([declaración de márgenes](../../fase1/declaracion-de-margenes.md)) and its answer is unwelcome — of fourteen rows, **two positive, seven negative, three undefined and two zero by construction**, one of the positives being a project choice. By magnitude the binding component is now the **escape window at −500 mm**; by consequence it is still the **section at −422 mm**, and the reason it has no margin is that every R2 option was solved *to* 4000 mm. So choosing an option that gives some back is a **regulatory requirement**, not a preference. The natural-hazard 1.4 factor is not a margin on the F2/S2 class and must not be double-counted. **And not one row clears the declaration's own adequacy test** — positive *and* larger than the uncertainty of the model that produced it — because in three rows the uncertainty is wider than the margin and in two the margin is zero by construction. See [adecuación](../../fase1/verificacion-incertidumbre.md).
- **Siting blockers (S024, S026):** three plot questions can each decide a site before the cabin does — whether the plot is in a mapped flood (1/200) or landslide (1/1000) zone under chapter 7; whether it lies **below the marine limit**, which forces a geotechnical investigation regardless of mapping; the **8.0 m** separation in §11-6; and the **50 m** fire-service access in §11-17. They belong in the Bergen site search, not in the later detail design.
