## 3. What mass adds — and what it does not

Every ratio above is per unit `W`, so the **ranking of layouts and verticals is mass-independent**: a heavier module does not make the corner layout better or worse, it scales every force in proportion. Mass therefore does not change the *choice*; it changes whether a given accessory or crane can carry the result. That is why C-07 blocks procurement rather than the geometry decision.

Per-leg tension in **tonnes**, two-effective-leg pattern (the case that governs accessory selection, D003), case I-1:

| Example mass kg | corner, 3000 mm | corner, 5000 mm | inset20, 3000 mm | inset20, 5000 mm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2000 | 1.50 | 1.20 | 1.27 | 1.10 |
| 3000 | 2.25 | 1.81 | 1.90 | 1.66 |
| 4000 | 3.00 | 2.41 | 2.54 | 2.21 |
| 5000 | 3.75 | 3.01 | 3.17 | 2.76 |
| 6000 | 4.50 | 3.61 | 3.81 | 3.31 |

**These masses are for scaling only. None of them is project data.** The useful reading is the spread rather than any single cell: at a fixed mass the difference between the worst and best geometry in that table is a factor of about **1.36**, which is what the layout and hook height are buying. A dynamic factor and the unequal-leg factor multiply all of it afterwards, and both belong to the crane company — they are not applied here.

Rigging self-weight is **not** included: it adds to the hook load but not to the module, so it must be added before any crane capacity check.

## 4. Interpretation

- The `inset20` layout shortens the plan radius, so for the same vertical it gives a steeper sling, lower tension and lower horizontal thrust than the extreme-corner layout. This is a starting point from SCI P272, not a justification for this chassis.
- At the same vertical, I-1 and I-2 have identical geometry ratios because vertical is measured from the lift point. The difference is structural: I-1 loads the upper frame; I-2 loads the chassis and is a below-CG critical lift under ESTA §3.4.1.
- Hook height above the lift point is the governing geometric variable. A low hook makes alpha small and multiplies both tension and horizontal thrust; the crane company must set the real geometry.
- Multiply `T/W` by the verified total weight only after C-07. Apply the specialist's dynamic factor and unequal-leg distribution before selecting any lifting accessory.
