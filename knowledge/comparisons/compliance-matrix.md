## Summary of blockers

1. **C-01…C-09 answers** — especially site/use classification, which decides the TEK17 chapter set and energy exceptions.
2. **Norwegian National Annex texts** — no γ, ψ or other coefficients may be used yet.
3. **NS 3031:2014** — not needed for the full §14-2 frame under the likely small detached/temporary routes, but may still be needed for heat-loss/thermal-bridge documentation; not held.
4. **Chapter 13 route finalisation** — applicable sections are retrieved in S023; C-01 classification and documented alternative ventilation quantities under §13-1(1) still needed.
5. **Written lift-plan paragraph** — §§10-1/10-2/10-3/13-1/13-3 verified, but the specific written-plan clause not located.
6. **NS-EN 1090-2:2018+A1:2024 full text** — no authorised source; execution class, welding and inspection cannot be fixed (S019).
7. **Mikrohus applicability** — DIBK rules verified but conditional on legal classification of the emergency cabin (S018).
8. **Fire strategy interpretation** — Chapter 11 requirements are verified in S024. The open questions have narrowed to three that are genuinely separate: whether the technical cupboard needs EI 30 separation; whether the whole cabin is treated as an escape route, which would raise interior surfaces from D-s2,d0 to B-s1,d0; and the §11-13 Table 1 travel distance for risk class 4, whose row was never retrieved. The roof branch no longer blocks (blocker 27), and the escape-window question is **priced** — but it is not the separate question it looked like: it is coupled to §11-14's door width (blocker 43).
9. **Escape-window geometry** — the R0 aperture table produces only one qualifying escape window (V-01); V-02 and V-03 fail §11-13(4). The revision is specified in `fase1/verificacion-ventanas.md` — V-02's sill drops to 1000 mm, V-03 goes to 900 × 600 at a 1000 mm sill on the bathroom gable — but the table itself is **unchanged**, because the revision is a design decision and not yet Carlos's.
10. **Roof insulation** — §11-4(3) no. 6 and §11-9 D no. 7 both require A2-s1,d0 insulation with an A2-s1,d0 substrate. A timber-rafter roof with wood-fibre insulation therefore needs a documented fire-engineering analysis, not a pre-accepted route.
11. **Chapter 7 natural hazards** — the requirements are now verified in S026 and the classes are fixed (**F2 1/200**, **S2 1/1000**) independently of C-01, but **no hazard map, flood level or marine-limit check has been done**. A Bergen plot can fail §7-2 or §7-3 before the cabin design matters, and below the marine limit a geotechnical investigation is required even outside mapped quick-clay zones. This is the first siting gate, ahead of §11-6 separation and §11-17 access. Screening method: `fase1/cribado-riesgos-naturales.md`.
12. **Room-height coverage fraction** — §12-7(2)(d) allows parts of a room below 2.2 m but states no required fraction. The integrated section solver (blocker 14) shows the position is much tighter than the first screen: at 20° with a 1100 mm stack, 250 mm floor and 300 mm roof, the eave cannot reach 2.2 m at all and only ~24 % of the width qualifies. The fraction is now a live design decision, not a formality.
13. **Envelope thickness against §14-3** — the wall needs about **189 mm** of mineral wool at a 15 % stud fraction, and the roof about 247 mm, once thermal bridging is counted. Those thicknesses compete with the 2.2 m room height and the 4.00 m transport limit. Generic material data only; no products selected. The chassis junction is now quantified in blocker 25.
14. **Integrated height, pitch and transport** — floor build-up, roof build-up (which costs **t/cos θ**), pitch and trailer stack all draw on the same 4.00 m. At the reference case the wall tops out at **1785 mm** and two of the five levers (floor and roof build-up) are arithmetically closed. The design has to choose between a shallower pitch, partial coverage, a lower stack or a dispensation, and that choice needs C-02 and C-06 together.
15. **No option has tolerance** — the four R2 options each solve exactly to the transport limit, and the numbers they were solved with are slightly optimistic: the envelope solver returns a floor **31 mm thicker** than the assumed 250 mm and a roof **22 mm thinner** than 300 mm, so the published wall heights are about **7 mm** high and the deficit is **−422 mm**, not −415. A **100 mm** reserve for ridge caps, overhangs or lifting ears takes R2-B from 24 % to **6 %** coverage and R2-D from 97 % to 79 %; only R2-A (8°) and R2-C (15°, 700 mm deck) survive intact. No option admits a sleeping loft. The choice is Carlos's and depends on how much the 20° gable is worth.
16. **Reclaimed timber has no documented route under §9-2** — every compliance instrument in the guidance (Svanemerket, ECOproduct, SINTEF TG, safety data sheet, EPD, CE declaration of conformity, manufacturer's written confirmation) presupposes a manufacturer, and reclaimed timber has none. Only a **professional assessment** remains, which means testing for lead paint, CCA or other preservative, PCB-bearing sealant and asbestos dust. This should be scoped **before the timber is bought**, and it is the real content of C-08.
17. **§9-3 is a second siting gate, independent of chapter 7** — chapter 7 asks whether nature threatens the building; §9-3 asks whether the ground threatens the occupants. A municipal **tiltaksplan** must be approved before any excavation, and the municipality may prohibit building outright. No ground investigation has been done and none can be done by the agent. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-instalaciones.md`.
18. **The waste-plan duties are decided by the project definition, not the design** — §9-6 cannot be triggered by building an 18 m² cabin at any plausible mass; the mass needed to cross the 10-tonne limb is 33–200 t. It **can** be triggered by §9-6(2) aggregation with a demolition, which is exactly what a reclaimed-timber supply chain does. §9-8 (70 wt% sorting) and §9-9 (final report) follow the same switch. Whether the project includes a demolition is a decision that belongs with C-08.
19. **The 0.90 m backflow head is an earthwork constraint** — §15-6(2) and §15-8(3) require the lowest trap to sit 0.90 m above the main pipe's internal top, which on a low chassis means a minimum drain cover of roughly **560 mm**. A municipal main satisfies it with ~940 mm to spare; a shallow sealed-tank connection fails by **160 mm**. The route out is a different drainage **concept** (raised floor, pumped tank), not a fitting, and the concept is unchosen. It pulls against the light-touch siting premise, §7-1(2) and §9-3 simultaneously.
20. **Chapters 2 and 4 are now carded (S029), with one provenance gap** — §1-2(2) admits chapters 1–7, so **chapter 2** (documentation for compliance) and **chapter 4** (FDV) apply in full. Both are now carded, and the **veiledning gap is closed** — [S030](../sources/S030.md) retrieved the guidance for §§2-2, 2-3, 2-4 and 4-2 plus the chapter 2 introduction on 2026-11-18. The pages render client-side and had returned navigation only; the text was in the DOM all along, collapsed behind a control that would not accept a click. **It was the last retrieval gap in the admitted scope, and it was not small**: it scoped the margin demand to analyses, named the absolute-krav reading, and prescribed the §2-2(4) overview the project did not have — which now exists as [`fase1/ytelsesoversikt.md`](../../fase1/ytelsesoversikt.md).
21. **§2-2(2) draws a line the project has been blurring** — analysis is available only where the regulation states a function and **no number**. Every fixed ytelse (the U-values, R 15, 2.2 m, 0.86 m, 0.90 m, 10 t, F2/S2, the 100-year basis, the daylight formula) must simply be met. The room-height fraction is an **analysis** and §2-2(3) governs it; the fire questions (is the roof main-system, does one escape window suffice) are **scope interpretations** and analysis cannot answer them — a determination can. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-documentacion.md`.
22. **§2-2(3) is the admissibility test for every screen, and the declaration now says the project is short of margin** — method validity, described assumptions, justified assumptions, stated safety margins. The first three are met across all thirteen reports. The fourth now has a single statement, [`fase1/declaracion-de-margenes.md`](../../fase1/declaracion-de-margenes.md), which fixes one currency per analysis type and tabulates fifteen rows: **two positive, seven negative, four undefined, two zero by construction**. One of the two positive rows is a project choice; the rest are negative, zero by construction or undefined — and the declaration's own adequacy test, which requires a margin to be positive **and larger than its uncertainty**, is cleared by **not one analysis** (nine rows are analyses; the scope correction is blocker 48). So the demand cannot be met by writing a number down — the design has no margin left, and R2 is the step that buys some. The natural-hazard 1.4 factor is **not** a margin on the F2/S2 class and must not be double-counted.
23. **§4-1(2)'s exemption is a dead end** — its examples are smaller garages, boathouses and outhouses: unheated, unserviced, non-dwelling. MiniHouse has all six FDV categories, including a wood stove and a wet room. Rejected as a determination. The FDV content itself is a short, closed list — six installation categories plus products/surfaces and instructions for use — and is the one part of the compliance picture that is finished in scope and merely needs filling.
24. **FDV must be versioned, not bound** — it has to stay *ajour* and match the building as built, but a re-sited cabin changes ground, flood level, wind/snow exposure, foundation and connections at every move. That is a documentation-architecture decision, cheap now and expensive later, and it is not taken.
25. **The chassis junction is unbounded by the regulation, and the benchmark is the wrong instrument** — §14-3 caps element U-values and airtightness and says **nothing** about linear bridges; the only figure, ≤0.05 W/m²K normalised, sits in §14-2's route, which §14-5(1) exempts. An 18 m² plan carries **61.10 m** of linear detail (3.39 m/m²), so ordinary detailing reads 0.210 W/m²K — 4.2× that benchmark and 16 % of heat loss. Compensating it with insulation costs +53 mm of wall and +65 mm of roof, and the roof share is **69 mm of eave height** against a 422 mm deficit: arithmetically open, geometrically shut. The benchmark itself implies a mean Ψ of 0.0147 W/mK, under half of good practice, because it is normalised by an area this building barely has — so any project target should be stated **per metre**, not per m². No Ψ has been calculated; no junction is drawn. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-puentes-termicas.md`.
26. **The junction is a moisture problem before it is an energy problem** — §14-3 caps heat and §13-13 caps vapour, and neither says anything about the **temperature of a steel member crossing the insulation line**. A 10 mm plate contributes 0.0002 m²K/W, so bare steel gives **f_Rsi 0.236** against the 0.754 the mould criterion requires, and its surface sits at **−2.9 °C** at −10 °C outside: wet, and frozen. It condenses whenever outdoors falls below **5.9 °C**, which on the Bergen coast is most of the heating season. The fix has a size, and **the 1D screen got the size and the surface wrong** — it priced **0.358 m²K/W**, roughly 9 mm of rigid foam, on the steel. **Mould governs, not condensation** (0.358 vs 0.193), and the **floor-to-wall corner** is the governing point because its film resistance is highest. The 2D calculation (blockers 38–41) keeps the conclusion, reverses the direction of the 1D error and moves the requirement to **0.80 m²K/W**. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-condensacion-union.md`.
27. **The roof interpretation question does not block the build-up** — S024 records that whether the roof is part of the main load-bearing system must be settled before the build-up is frozen. It does not: all four branches of §11-4(3) — no. 4 R 15, and no. 6.1, 6.2 and 6.3 — require **A2-s1,d0 insulation**, because §11-9 D's main rule applies independently and no. 7's escape route needs a load-bearing substrate meeting A2-s1,d0, which a timber rafter is not. So the insulation is **mineral wool** whatever the structural scheme, and the build-up can be frozen now. **No. 6.2 is closed** — it demands a wholly non-combustible roof. What stays open is the **rafter** and the **lining**, and both are answered by one lining. Determination: `fase1/determinacion-cubierta-incendio.md`.
28. **R 15 is a charring problem, and the ordinary 48 mm rafter does not survive it** — if the roof is main-system, EN 1995-1-2 gives a 15-minute effective char depth of **15.0 mm** per face, and a rafter exposed on three sides loses the depth twice across its width: **48 × 198 retains 32 %** of its section modulus, and going deeper does not help. Parity needs **78 mm**, so in practice **98 mm** stock. The 48 mm rafter fails at every swept accidental load, and the load is swept because no structural model and no C-07 exist. The way out is a protective lining — which is also what no. 6.3 requires — or a rafter that doubles in width. **The fire branch costs about 40 mm of eave height**, because wider rafters raise the timber fraction.
29. **The stud fraction is a geometry result, not a material property** — this corrects the envelope screen's basis. The fraction is **member width ÷ centres**, so the uniform 15 % implies **90 mm** members. At 48 mm on 600 mm centres the roof field needs **214 mm**, not 245 mm — but sole and top plates, noggins and jack studs push the effective fraction back up, so the honest relief is **13–31 mm** of thickness (14–33 mm of eave height), not a fixed 31 mm. The project should state width and centres, not a fraction. Screening: `fase1/determinacion-cubierta-incendio.md`.
30. **R2-D's 4400 mm dispensation is 22 mm short of what it needs** — full coverage at 20° and a 1100 mm stack requires **4415 mm** even at the published build-ups, and **4422 mm** at a 15 % timber fraction. So the 4400 mm in the option table was never the threshold for full coverage; it was a round number that landed at 97 %. At a 12 % effective fraction 4400 mm suffices, and at 8 % only **4358 mm** is needed. **The number written in the application is the number that gets assessed**, so asking for 4400 when 4422 is required invites a refusal that a slightly slimmer frame would have avoided. Screening: `fase1/opciones-seccion-R2.md`.
31. **The timber fraction is the only lever on the section that costs nothing at the transport limit** — re-solving the four options across 15 %, 12 % and 8 % moves R2-B from 23 % to **34 %** coverage and R2-D from 96 % to **100 %**, and reduces the dispensation needed from 4422 to **4358 mm**. It is worth 28–64 mm of eave height, an order of magnitude more than any build-up correction. It depends on the structural scheme, so it is not a lever anyone can pull yet.
32. **§11-13 has three readings and the aperture table passes one of them** — §11-13(1) needs one exit to a safe place, and the door discharges to grade; §11-13(4) then relaxes the two-direction rule *provided the fire cell has «vinduer»*, plural. So whether the cabin needs escape windows at all depends on whether the door already reaches a safe place — a determination, not a calculation. Reading A takes the door as sufficient and needs nothing. Reading B engages (4) and needs two qualifying windows in the cell. Reading C asks for a qualifying window in every room without a direct exit, which is the **bathroom**, whose only opening is V-03 at 500 × 500 with a 1500 mm sill. The three are priced in `fase1/verificacion-ventanas.md`: A costs nothing, B costs a **100 mm** sill drop on V-02 at no cost in glass, C costs **0.29 m²** on V-03. Designing to the union means the municipality's answer cannot break the design, which is worth more than the 0.29 m².
33. **The sentence that makes the aperture table look compliant is about a different requirement** — the dimensions table in S024 carries «one accessible escape window is sufficient in a dwelling unit». It sits under *fire-service access* and refers to the window the fire service can reach under §11-17; it is **not** a relaxation of «vinduer» in §11-13(4). Read as one, the R0 table passes with V-01 alone. It should not be read that way, and the ambiguity is worth putting to the municipality in the same letter as C-01 rather than resolving privately in the favourable direction.
34. **The escape-window deficit is the largest dimensional one in the project, and the cheapest to close** — under the strictest reading V-03 is **−500 mm** short on the sum rule and on the sill, and V-02 is −100 mm on the sill. That is larger than the section's −422 mm, so by magnitude the escape window is now the binding component of the margin vector; by consequence it is not, because it closes by **enlarging a hole** while the section closes only by a dispensation or a shallower pitch. The same change is also the daylight fix: enlarging V-03 takes qualifying glass from 2.47 to 2.76 m² and the minimum light transmittance from **0.600 to 0.537** at a frame factor of 0.85 — the only lever in the project that moves two requirements at once. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-ventanas.md`.
35. **The bathroom window cannot go where it is drawn** — V-03 sits on the y=0 wall, and the bathroom's back wall is only **1200 mm** long, so a 900 mm aperture leaves **150 mm** piers: too thin for a weathertight corner return and for the frame fixings. The bathroom's gable at x=0 is **3000 mm** and takes the opening comfortably. Moving it costs nothing in daylight, because the §13-7 count is orientation-blind. What is not settled is what sits under the window: §11-13 allows a 1.0 m sill «unless measures ease escape», and a WC or shower tray under the opening raises the effective sill.
36. **Not one declared margin is adequate, by the declaration's own definition** — §2-2(3) asks for margins that are *necessary*, and the declaration defines adequate as positive **and larger than the uncertainty of the model that produced it**. `fase1/verificacion-incertidumbre.md` populates that column and runs the test on the nine rows where §2-2(3) requires a margin: **0 adequate, 5 not adequate, 4 untestable**, with six rows outside the requirement by analysis type and none of them thereby satisfied. It separates four kinds of uncertainty — model, input, interpretation and construction — because only the second is reducible by gathering data. Three findings matter more than the count. **Row 2's +40 mm is not a margin at all**: it is a nominal 900 mm leaf minus a frame and hinge build-up nobody has specified, and ordinary door sets spend 30–60 mm on it, so the sign is unknown. **Two rows are zero by construction** — the transport envelope and the wall U — because both dimensions were *solved* to their limit; the declaration admitted only the first. And **row 5's −422 mm is mid-band**: at 23 % timber fraction the deficit is **−498 mm**, so the project has been quoting the 15 % value where a worst case belongs.
37. **The uncertainty is not a footnote on the margin, it is larger than the margin in three rows** — the wall U moves by **0.051 W/m²K** across the plausible framing band, the bridges by **0.28** across the detailing levels, the section by **139 mm** across the timber fractions. Only the section's sign survives its own uncertainty. In the other two the sign is set by a choice, not by the physics, which means the rows cannot be read as passes or failures until the choice is made.
38. **The 1D condensation requirement was sized on a surface that stops governing as soon as it is built** — every f_Rsi in this repository was a one-dimensional number, and the floor-edge junction has now been solved in 2D (`fase1/verificacion-puente-2d.md`, ISO 10211, finite volumes with Robin films, validated against 1D to 0.002 and flux to 1e-6). The steel plate gives **0.377**, not the 0.236 the 1D path gave, and the reason matters: lateral feed from the surrounding floor warms the member. But the steel is the coldest point **only while it is bare**. From the first millimetre of pad the coldest point moves to the **timber wall corner** and stays there for every thickness, so the requirement is **0.80 m²K/W (20 mm of foam)**, not 0.358 (9 mm). Nine millimetres gives f_Rsi **0.693** against a criterion of 0.754. The declaration's row 13 therefore read **+0.051 where the 2D answer is −0.39**, and the sign was inverted rather than merely uncertain.
39. **Padding the steel is the wrong fix and the numbers are not close** — putting the same 10 mm plate **below the floor, outside the insulation** gives f_Rsi **0.921** and Ψ **+0.027**, identical to the bare timber corner to three decimals, with no pad, no extra material and no thickness. Padding never gets there: 60 mm of foam reaches 0.863 and is still climbing. Over 18 m of floor perimeter the difference between the two positions is **3.01 W/K**, about **16 %** of the envelope's element heat loss (19.4 W/K at the §14-3 limits). One drawing decision — *run the insulation outside the steel* — moves the heat balance by more than every insulation choice in this project combined, and it is the only place where the energy and moisture answers coincide.
40. **The generic bridge table is now bracketed, and its conclusion survives its own number** — that screen assumed **0.10 W/mK** for the floor perimeter; 2D gives **0.027** wrapped and **0.195** unwrapped. The generic value sits between them, which confirms rather than corrects the bridge screen: its conclusion was that junctions must be *designed* rather than estimated, and its number could not be improved without a detail. What changes is the priority — the floor-edge junction is not one of 61.10 m of similar inventory, it is the one that decides whether the steel is inside or outside the warm layer.
41. **A plate is not a chassis member, and that is what stops this being a design value** — no A, W, I, J, thickness or grade exists for the chassis (C-07), so the 2D model puts 10 mm of steel in the worst credible place and reads the *difference* between variants. A real member has a top flange in contact with the floor over its full width, which will read better than the plate and worse than the wrapped case. The 3D corner, the perimeter joist and the vapour field are all outside the model. The 2D result is an instrument, not a junction detail.
42. **The perimeter joist is an element problem wearing a junction's clothes** — a joist parallel to the wall, under the deck and through the full insulation depth, is the one joist a 2D section can see, and it is the perimeter trimmer that bears on the chassis beam. Raw, it appears to add **+0.014 W/mK** to the junction at every position inside the modelled window. It does not: the floor's element U rises from **0.1363** to **0.1716** at the 12 % timber fraction the modelled window contains, and that increase alone accounts for the whole shift — referenced to the joisted floor U the junction reads **+0.028**, which is the unjoisted corner. **A 2D junction model and a parallel-path element U must not both count the same joist**, and this is the same error family as the stud fraction: a quantity that is not a property of the material being carried as one. What the joist costs at the junction is small — f_Rsi 0.921 → **0.906** — because the 22 mm deck spreads over it. The joist **perpendicular** to the wall crosses the perimeter as a **point** once every spacing and is a 3D effect this section cannot see. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-puente-2d.md`.
43. **The escape windows and the door width are one determination, not two** — §11-13(4) is written as an **alternative**: the exit from the fire cell leads *either* to a safe place, *or* to a rømningsvei with a single escape direction and then the windows are required. §11-14 governs the rømningsvei and doors in or to it. So if the door discharges to a safe place, (4) is **not engaged** and §11-14 has **nothing to govern** — which means declaration rows **2, 3 and 4** all stop being requirements at once, and the tally drops from 2 positive / 6 negative to 1 positive / 4 negative over ten rows. If the exit leads to a rømningsvei, all three are mandatory and buildable for **0.29 m²** of glass and a 100 mm sill. The project has been carrying them as three independent prices. **What is not coupled is §11-9**: a fire cell that contains no rømningsvei is still a fire cell, so **D-s2,d0** governs walls and ceiling whatever §11-13 decides — which is the answer that keeps the reclaimed timber exposed, and coupling the two would have condemned the interior on a reading that does not reach it. Travel distance inside the cabin is **5.04 m** against the 15 m figure the retrieved text contains, but the applicable Table 1 row is still missing. Determination: `fase1/determinacion-estrategia-incendio.md`.
44. **A mechanical extract in a sealed cabin does not move air — it depressurises the cabin** — §13-1(1) requires documented alternative air quantities, and the project had recorded that sentence without ever producing them. [The ventilation screen](../../fase1/verificacion-ventilacion.md) produces them: **52 m³/h** governing supply against a **90 m³/h** basic and **108–216 m³/h** boosted extract. But a fan in a sealed 36.9 m³ box only moves what the envelope leaks, and at the §14-3 airtightness the fitted extract holds the cabin at **106–140 Pa** of negative pressure — against a chimney draught of **10–20 Pa**, so the flue reverses by **−130 Pa**. The both-rooms-boosted case needs 406 Pa, more than a fan develops, so that flow is simply not delivered. This is the only **safety** margin in the declaration and it is negative for a reason no insulation choice touches: the make-up air path. Every answer to that is a hole in the vapour barrier row 13 depends on — **rows 13 and 14 are the same drawing**. It also engages §13-5: sustained negative pressure is the mechanism that draws soil gas through the floor. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-ventilacion.md`.

45. **The make-up air has a size, and it does not buy the flue** — the ventilation screen ended on a decision, not a number; [the make-up air screen](../../fase1/verificacion-aporte-aire.md) supplies it. An inlet of area A passes `Q = 3600·Cd·A·√(2Δp/ρ)`, so holding the cabin at a chosen depression while the design extract of 108 m³/h runs fixes A: **113 mm** at 10 Pa, which is one wall valve and takes 98 Pa out of the 106 Pa the basic extract would otherwise carry. The ventilation and the §13-5 driving force are solved by that hole, **and the flue is not**: at 10 Pa the margin against a 10–20 Pa draught is 0 to +10 Pa, and the only depression that passes the §2-2(3) adequacy test — margin positive *and* larger than its uncertainty — is 2 Pa, which needs a **181 mm** inlet and is below the pressure a 3 m/s breeze puts on a wall. **A passive inlet cannot hold a set pressure at all.** The finding survives dropping the size limit: the wind, not the 125 mm ceiling, excludes every adequate row. So **row 14 is retired, not improved** — a **room-sealed** stove balances its flue against the outside and removes the coupling, which is a specification requirement with no dimensional cost, and the only negative row in the declaration with one. Two further numbers: the make-up air costs **905 W** at 30 K, **1.65×** the fabric loss of **18.3 W/K**, so heat recovery is a precondition rather than an upgrade; and by diffusion the hole carries **0.8 %** of the barrier's conductance — the risk is convective, so the inlet is a **control problem** (a closable vapour-tight valve), not a diffusion one. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-aporte-aire.md`.

46. **§13-5 radon: the floor is not on the ground, and the clause assumes it is** — §13-5 asks for a radon barrier **against the ground** plus preparation for pressure reduction above 100 Bq/m³, unless documented unnecessary. This floor sits on a ventilated void over the chassis, so the prescriptive route describes a detail the building does not contain. [The radon screen](../../fase1/verificacion-radon.md) assesses what can be assessed without a site, and finds a bound that no void or membrane can exceed: **C ≤ E/Q**, the pathological case where every atom exhaled under the footprint reaches the room. On ordinary ground that is **3.6–36 Bq/m³** against a 200 limit — **5.6× inside it** at the top of the range — because 90 m³/h of ventilation passes through an 18 m² footprint. The void can only improve on the bound, and even a skirted one turns it over 19–32 times an hour, because a shallow void has a small volume and a long perimeter. **Alum shale breaks it**: at 300 mBq/(m²·s) the bound is **216 Bq/m³**, over the limit, so **C-01 decides the sign and the arithmetic does not**. Two consequences: a radon barrier is an **airtightness** item, not an Sd item, so the Sd ≥ 10 m vapour barrier does not satisfy it by construction; and the barrier's continuity is the **floor-to-wall junction**, 18 m of the same corner the 2D screen solved — one drawing answers heat, vapour and radon, or none of them is answered. The make-up air inlet is in the *other* membrane; the drain is the wet penetration that needs a sealed collar. Screening: `fase1/verificacion-radon.md`.

47. **The junction finally has a drawing, and it is generated rather than drawn** — five screens now land on one corner: heat (f_Rsi ≥ 0.754), vapour (Sd ≥ 10 m), airtightness and radon (§13-5's barrier), structure (the chassis beam) and moisture (the drain). No drawing of it existed, and the planos in `fase1/planos/` are hand-authored SVG — the failure mode this project has paid for four times. [The junction detail](../../fase1/planos/detalle-encuentro-R0.svg) is generated from `puente_2d`'s own constants, and its self-test fails if the drawn geometry stops matching them. What it settles is the question prose could not: **the barrier turns the corner at (13, −22)**, behind the lining and above the insulation, and the chassis plate **crosses that plane when it is not wrapped**. So the same drawing decision removes the thermal bridge (f_Rsi 0.377 → 0.921) and leaves the barrier continuous — wrapping is not an insulation thickness, it is an instruction. The drawing also shows the void the radon screen says must not be closed, and the drain as the one wet penetration through the floor. **Conceptual, not for fabrication.**

48. **The margin test never applied to all fifteen rows, and the four that leave it enter a stricter regime** — §2-2(3) reads as though every analysis must state margins, and [the declaration](../../fase1/declaracion-de-margenes.md) applied it uniformly for two revisions. The veiledning says otherwise: «Hvilke sikkerhetsmarginer er lagt til grunn (**nødvendig ved mer omfattende analyse**)», and «en enkel faglig vurdering eller et logisk resonnement» may be enough. So **nine** rows are analyses and carry the margin duty, **four** (2, 3, 4, 10) are stated ytelser that carry no analysis at all, and **two** (6, 9) are determinations. The correction is conservative in one direction and severe in the other. Conservative: no analysis is relieved — 0 adequate, 5 not adequate, 4 untestable, unchanged. Severe: a stated ytelse must simply be met, and §2-2(1)'s guidance allows a deviation **only by municipal dispensation** under PBL ch. 19, on a test of «klart større fordeler enn ulemper». Rows 3 and 4 are **−500 mm** against a stated aperture figure, so they are not two thin negative margins — they are **two deviations that need a dispensation**, which is harder to obtain than a positive margin, not easier. Row 2's +40 mm was never the project's thinnest margin either; it is an undimensioned free width. The same correction carries the **absolute-krav** reading from the chapter 2 introduction — a numeric level is absolute unless a tolerance is granted, and NS 3420 tolerances govern measured execution rather than design — so a **margin is not a tolerance**, and solving to 4000 mm spends one nobody granted. Source: [S030](../../knowledge/sources/S030.md). Screening: `fase1/verificacion-incertidumbre.md`.

49. **The ytelsesoversikt exists, and it says the building discharges nothing** — §2-2(4) requires an overview of the ytelser relied on, and the veiledning prescribes its three categories. [The overview](../../fase1/ytelsesoversikt.md) assembles **38 rows**: 27 stated in the forskrift, **0 preaccepted**, 4 documented by analysis, and 7 listed explicitly *outside* the overview because they are not ytelser at all (a benchmark that does not bind, a road-transport constraint, two project choices, a lifting capacity, a partial factor and a §12-7(2)(d) relief). Every value is read from the screen that owns it — `ec.U_LIMITS`, `rc.RADON_LIMIT_BQ`, `fc.R_MAIN_MIN`, `vc.N50_PER_H`, `ic.BACKFLOW_HEAD_MM` — so a constant cannot move without the overview following, and the self-test fails if a declared margin stops being cited. Three findings the screens could not show on their own: **(a) the preaccepted category is empty and that is structural** — a building with no foundation, no site-assembled envelope and no conventional roof has no preaccepted route, so §2-2(2)(b)'s analysis route is the only route and §2-2(3) carries the whole load; **(b) not one of the 31 ytelser is discharged** — 9 are not met, 20 undetermined, 2 do not apply — which is what R0 means, since a screening is not a verification; **(c) the queue is longer than the list looked** — 21 distinct decisions close these rows and **17 close exactly one**, so the comfortable «four decisions unblock the building» is false: C-01 closes 4, a product 3, R2 3, and the rest are one-offs. Two rows looked like they waited on work rather than a decision — §14-3's airtightness and §15-5's services design — and **blocker 50 shows the first of those is not cheap in time**. Generated by `scripts/ytelsesoversikt.py`, check 33.

50. **The airtightness test is specified, and specifying it turned the row inside out** — [the test specification](../../fase1/especificacion-ensayo-tetthet.md) is the first artefact in this project that is neither a screen nor a determination but a *plan to measure*, and writing it changed three things the ytelsesoversikt could not see. **(a) n50 is volume-normalised and this building is envelope-heavy.** Envelope **66.48 m²** over **36.92 m³** gives A/V **1.80 m⁻¹** against ~0.77 for a compact two-storey house, so the same 1.5 h⁻¹ demands q50 **0.83 m³/(h·m²)** — **2.3×** lower — across **61.10 m** of linear detail, or **0.91 m³/h per metre of joint** at 50 Pa. The limit is trivial in absolute flow (55 m³/h, one small fan) and demanding per square metre, which is why the test is worth buying and why the drawings cannot answer it. **(b) The envelope has a floor as well as a ceiling.** §14-3 rewards tightness; the extract needs to be fed. In a sealed cabin the depression rises as leakage falls — 106 Pa at 1.50 h⁻¹, **149 Pa at 1.20**, 151 Pa at 1.19 — so below **1.19 h⁻¹** the 90 m³/h basic extract cannot be delivered at a 150 Pa fan at all. That is not a requirement (the inlet holds 10 Pa and n50 never enters) but it is the **fault case**, and it is why the inlet must be interlocked rather than left to the occupant: a closed inlet leaves the cabin at 106 Pa at 1.50 and 149 Pa at 1.20. **So «as tight as we can build» is not the instruction**; the target is **1.30 h⁻¹**, a band rather than a minimum. **(c) The acceptance threshold is not the limit.** The declaration's own rule — a margin is adequate only if positive *and* larger than its uncertainty — applied to a measurement puts acceptance at **1.36** for a 10 % test and **1.43** for a 5 % one, so a poor test can fail a cabin built to target: **the quality of the measurement is part of the acceptance**. Two by-products: the inlet is **113→116 mm** across the whole band, so it can be **ordered before the measurement**; and the test returns the leakage exponent the screens assume (0.65), worth **40 Pa** on the ventilation screen's headline number between 0.50 and 0.80. **The row stays `undetermined`** — a specification is not a measurement, exactly as a screening is not a verification — and the standard that defines the method, **NS-EN ISO 9972:2015**, is **not held** (S031). Generated by `scripts/tetthet_test.py`, check 34.
