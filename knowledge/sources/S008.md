- **Authors:** R M Lawson, P J Grubb, J Prewer, P J Trebilcock
- **Publisher:** The Steel Construction Institute, Ascot, UK. SCI Publication P272.
- **URL:** https://steelconstruction.info/upload/Wiki-Files/f/fd/SCI_P272.pdf
- **Full text:** `knowledge/refs/SCI_P272.pdf` (105 pages, free — downloaded 2026-11-16)
- **Access:** free PDF

## Key claims relevant to MiniHouse

- Modular units' light steel components are designed for normal structural actions **plus additional forces due to lifting and other construction operations**.
- "The structure may be 'over-engineered' for its normal applications, as sizing is more dictated by stability and rigidity in the lifting and transportation operations."
- A heavy lifting frame equal to the plan dimensions is preferred because it "does not cause horizontal or shear forces in the units".
- **"The optimum lifting points are at 20% of the length of the unit in from both ends, so that the structure is most stable."** For a 6 m module that is **1.2 m from each end**.
- Units are often lifted from corners, which "generally necessitates the use of a separate lifting frame or pairs of cross-beams".
- Lifting from the base is possible for small units, but "the change of angle of the lifting cables over the top of the module may cause local damage to the upper corner of the units" — directly relevant to MiniHouse's base-lift variant I-2.
- Lifting forces must include a **dynamic component as a multiple of self weight**.
- Forces are normally distributed equally among four lifting points, but the design must also include horizontal forces from sloping cables.
- Units should be weather-tight during transport (wind buffeting damage); heavy-duty plastic wrap typical.
- Robustness: remaining structure must survive loss of one element without disproportionate damage; walls either span as deep beam/diaphragm or are tied horizontally and vertically to adjacent units.

## Important nuance resolved

The research report warns that "1.2 m" is not an optimal **support** position for the ideal beam — correct. SCI P272 independently recommends 20% inset (1.2 m on 6 m) as the optimal **lifting point** position for stability. These are different questions and both statements can be true. Do not conflate them; record the distinction in the design reasoning.

## Limits and caveats

- UK transport limits in §7.1 (3.5 m general / 4.3 m with route permission / 2.9 m special types) are **UK rules, not Norwegian**. MiniHouse uses the Norwegian 2.55–3.25 m divisible-load band from `fase1/registro-decisiones.md` instead.
- Light steel framing focus; MiniHouse's primary structure is a steel chassis with heavier members.
- Published 2001 — check current SCI editions for any updated handling guidance.
