# S027 · TEK17 Chapter 9 — external environment

- **Publisher:** DIBK, Norway
- **Access:** free, official; retrieved 2026-11-17
- **Base index:** https://www.dibk.no/regelverk/byggteknisk-forskrift-tek17/9
- **Sections:** §9-1 … §9-10 (chapter has no roman-numeral segment)
- **Scope driver:** TEK17 §1-2(2) admits chapters 1–7, **9–11**, 14, 15–17 on the mikrohus route (S020). Chapter 9 is therefore admitted **in full**, with no sub-provision carve-out.

## A structural correction before anything else

Chapter 9 is routinely assumed to be the "noise, water and sewage" chapter. It is not.
Reading the section list settles it:

| § | Heading | Actually about |
|---|---|---|
| §9-1 | Generelle krav til ytre miljø | general environmental duty |
| §9-2 | Helse- og miljøfarlige stoffer | hazardous substances **in products** |
| §9-3 | Forurensning i grunnen | contaminated **ground** |
| §9-4 | Utvalgte naturtyper | selected nature types |
| §9-5 | Byggavfall og ombruk | construction waste and **reuse** |
| §9-6 | Avfallsplan | waste plan |
| §9-7 | Kartlegging av farlig avfall … | mapping, **existing buildings only** |
| §9-8 | Avfallssortering | waste sorting |
| §9-9 | Sluttrapport | final waste report |
| §9-10 | Utslippskrav til vedovner | **wood-stove emissions** |

There is **no noise provision** (that is §13-6, and §13-6 is exempt on this route),
**no water or sewage provision** (those are chapter 15), and **no outdoor air
provision** beyond the stove rule. So the chapter's real weight for MiniHouse sits in
three places: **§9-2 and the reclaimed timber**, **§9-3 as a second siting gate**, and
**§9-10 plus §15-3 as the wood-stove cluster**.

## §9-1 — general requirement

> «Byggverk skal prosjekteres, oppføres, driftes og rives på en måte som medfører minst
> mulig belastning på naturressurser og det ytre miljøet. Byggavfallet skal håndteres
> tilsvarende.»

No numbers. The guidance asks for a **miljøprogram** (environmental targets) and a
**miljøoppfølgingsplan** (follow-up measures), both prepared at the start and scaled to
the size and complexity of the project — it states no threshold. The theme list follows
**NS 3466:2009**, and the guidance points every theme at the section that governs it.

For an 18 m² cabin the programme is short, but the duty exists and is **admitted in
full**. It is also the provision that makes "reclaimed timber" a *declared* choice rather
than an incidental one.

## §9-2 — hazardous substances in products

> «Det skal velges produkter uten eller med lavt innhold av helse- eller miljøfarlige
> stoffer.»

One sentence, and it is the sharpest constraint on the reclaimed-timber strategy in the
whole regulation. The guidance names the substance families that count — **CMR**
(carcinogenic, mutagenic, reproductive-toxic), **PBT**, **vPvB** — and the two lists that
operationalise them: the **Norwegian Priority List** (Miljødirektoratet) and the **REACH
Candidate List** (SVHC). It then adds the substitution duty of **produktkontrolloven §3a**:
a business using a product that may harm health or the environment must investigate
whether a less risky alternative exists and must take it if that is possible without
unreasonable cost or disadvantage.

**The compliance instruments are all manufacturer instruments.** The guidance gives an
explicit hierarchy, and every rung presupposes a supplier:

1. pre-assessed products — **Svanemerket / EU Ecolabel**, **ECOproduct** (avoid red in
   "2.1 Helse og miljøfarlige stoffer"), **SINTEF Teknisk Godkjenning**;
2. if not pre-assessed — **sikkerhetsdatablad**, **EPD** (Norwegian EPDs from **2012**
   onward state REACH Candidate List content), **CE declaration of conformity** for
   construction products;
3. failing all of that — a **written confirmation from the manufacturer's legally
   responsible person** that the product contains no Priority List or Candidate List
   substance.

**Reclaimed timber has no manufacturer.** It has no EPD, no CE marking, no declaration of
conformity, no safety data sheet, and no legally responsible person to sign a
confirmation. The **SCIP** duty in produkforskriften §2-32 — notification at **>0.1 weight
percent** SVHC — falls on *suppliers of articles*, and a private party harvesting timber
from a demolished building is not one.

So §9-2 does not forbid reclaimed timber. It makes the **pre-accepted documentation route
structurally unavailable**, leaving only the route the guidance also contemplates: a
**professional assessment and comparison** of hazardous-substance content. For old timber
that means answering, per element, whether it carries lead paint, CCA or other
preservative treatment, PCB-containing sealants, or asbestos-contaminated dust — which is
a **testing** question, not a paperwork question.

That is the real content of C-08, and it is now a documented regulatory gap rather than a
general worry.

## §9-3 — contaminated ground

> «Ved planlegging av byggverk skal det undersøkes om det finnes forurenset grunn.»

One sentence, and it opens a **second siting gate** independent of chapter 7. The
mechanism:

- the **tiltakshaver** investigates natural and environmental conditions **before applying
  for a permit**, and confirms sufficient safety in the application per **SAK10 §5-4(g)**;
- **forurensningsforskriften chapter 2** imposes an *independent* duty to assess whether
  the ground is contaminated;
- on suspicion, the extent must be clarified;
- if the ground **is** contaminated, a **tiltaksplan** per **forurensningsforskriften §2-6**
  must be **approved by the municipality before building or excavation starts**;
- the municipality may **prohibit** construction or attach conditions to the ground, the
  buildings and the outdoor area.

Typical suspects named by the guidance: central urban areas, and land next to **abandoned
waste facilities or industrial sites**. Bergen's central districts and its harbour-side
industrial history make this a live question, not a formality — and note the asymmetry
with chapter 7: chapter 7 asks whether *nature* threatens the building, §9-3 asks whether
the *ground* is a hazard to health. A plot can pass one and fail the other.

## §9-4 — selected nature types

Applies only where **all three** conditions hold:

1. a regulation under **naturmangfoldloven §§52 and 53(5)** designates the nature type;
2. occurrences exist **in the municipality**; and
3. the relationship is **not already settled in a legally binding plan**.

Then (a) siting and design must take special care to avoid deteriorating the type's
distribution and ecological condition, and (b) if pbl. chapter 4 impact assessment has not
clarified the consequences, the developer must produce a **konsekvensanalyse** limited to
that nature type. The guidance is explicit that (b) is a **procedural rule from which
dispensation cannot be granted**.

Two practical notes. The rule plays a **secondary** role because the municipality is meant
to settle it in the plan — it bites where the plan predates the regulation or is too
coarse (kommuneplan only, no reguleringsplan). And **size is not a criterion**: an 18 m²
cabin is caught exactly like a tower block if the three conditions hold. This is a
plot-level question answerable from the naturbase register plus the plan status.

## §9-5 — construction waste and reuse

> «(1) Byggverket skal sikres en forsvarlig og tilsiktet levetid slik at avfallsmengden
> over byggverkets livsløp begrenses til et minimum.»
>
> «(2) Det skal velges produkter som er egnet for ombruk og materialgjenvinning. Byggverk
> skal prosjekteres og bygges slik at det er tilrettelagt for senere demontering når dette
> kan gjennomføres innenfor en praktisk og økonomisk forsvarlig ramme.»

This is the provision that most directly *rewards* the MiniHouse concept, and it does so
in language that is almost a specification for it.

Paragraph (1) is about **service life** — durable materials, protective construction
(especially moisture-safe), accurate execution, correct storage, and buildings that are
easy to convert.

Paragraph (2) is about **design for disassembly**, and the guidance supplies the design
rules:

- the considerations on reuse, material recycling and preparation for later disassembly
  **must be documented in the prosjektering** — a documentation duty, not just an intention;
- products containing **hazardous substances**, and material types that are **difficult to
  separate**, are **poorly suited** for reuse and recycling;
- elements must be dismantleable **without being substantially altered**, and **how they
  are fastened is decisive**;
- NS 3466:2009, **ISO 20887:2020** (design for disassembly and adaptability) and the DOK
  regulation are the reference frame.

**MiniHouse is the rare building that satisfies (2) by its own logic** — it is designed to
be lifted whole, so its primary structure is already a demountable assembly. But the
guidance's warning cuts both ways: a chassis welded to a timber frame, or a roof membrane
bonded to sheathing, is *difficult to separate* and therefore *poorly suited* for reuse,
whatever the marketing says. The **fastening strategy is the reuse strategy**, and it is a
design decision that has not yet been taken.

## §9-6 — waste plan: the 10-tonne switch

> «(1) For følgende tiltak skal det utarbeides en avfallsplan … a) oppføring, tilbygging,
> påbygging og underbygging av bygningen dersom tiltaket overskrider 300 m² BRA … b)
> vesentlig endring … mer enn 100 m² BRA … c) riving … overskrider 100 m² BRA … d)
> oppføring, tilbygging, påbygging, underbygging, endring eller riving av bygninger,
> konstruksjoner og anlegg dersom tiltaket genererer over 10 tonn bygg- og rivningsavfall.»
>
> «(2) Tiltak som omfatter flere bygninger, konstruksjoner eller anlegg skal vurderes
> under ett.»

| Limb | Trigger | MiniHouse |
|---|---|---|
| (a) new build | **>300 m² BRA** | 18 m² → **no** |
| (b) alteration / repair | **>100 m² BRA** | not an alteration → **no** |
| (c) demolition | **>100 m² BRA** | not a demolition → **no** |
| (d) any of the above | **>10 tonnes** of construction and demolition waste | **unknown — depends on mass and on how the timber is sourced** |

So the entire avfallsplan duty hangs on **one limb, and it is a mass limb**. For a new-build
cabin the area limbs are not close; the tonnage limb is the only live question.

Paragraph (2) is the trap. Where a project comprises several buildings or installations
they are assessed **as a whole**. If the MiniHouse project includes **dismantling a source
building to harvest reclaimed timber**, that demolition waste aggregates with the cabin's
waste, and a 10-tonne threshold that looked comfortable for an 18 m² cabin becomes easy to
cross. The reclaimed-timber strategy and the waste-plan duty are therefore coupled: the
more the project leans on reclaimed material, the more likely limb (d) engages.

The plan must state expected **quantities and types** by waste type, and how each is to be
handled or disposed of; waste going **directly to reuse or recycling** must also be listed.
Responsibility follows **SAK10 §§12-2 to 12-4**; the final document is form **5178**.

## §9-7 — mapping: existing buildings only

> «(1) For tiltak i eksisterende byggverk skal det foretas kartlegging av bygningsdeler,
> installasjoner og lignende som kan utgjøre farlig avfall etter avfallsforskriften.»

The guidance is unambiguous: paragraph (1) covers **all** interventions in existing
buildings — endring, reparasjon, riving under pbl. §20-1 — and is **not** limited to the
§9-6(b)–(d) categories. Paragraphs (2)–(5) add the **miljøkartleggingsrapport** and the
**ombrukskartleggingsrapport**, the latter only for existing **boligblokk** or
**yrkesbygning**.

**A new-build MiniHouse is outside §9-7 entirely.** No environmental mapping report, no
reuse mapping report. This is a clean exemption and worth stating plainly, because the
instinct on a reclaimed-material project is the opposite.

Note the boundary, though: the exemption covers the *cabin*. If the project dismantles
another building to obtain the timber, that building is an existing building and §9-7
applies to **it** — including the declaration form (deklarasjonsskjema) required before
hazardous waste leaves the site, per avfallsforskriften chapter 11.

## §9-8 — sorting: 70 weight percent

> «Minimum 70 vektprosent av avfallet som oppstår i tiltak etter § 9-6 første ledd skal
> sorteres i rene avfallstyper, og alt avfall skal leveres til godkjent avfallsmottak,
> ombruk eller direkte til gjenvinning.»

Scope is defined by reference: only projects caught by **§9-6 first paragraph**. Since
§9-6 is not engaged by area and may or may not be engaged by tonnage, **§9-8 inherits
exactly the same switch**. If the 10-tonne limb is not crossed, there is no 70 % duty.

Where it does apply, three guidance points matter:

- sorting is expected **on site**; the plot must therefore be given space for it;
- **central sorting is the exception**, and sorting all waste centrally will *normally not*
  satisfy the requirement;
- **"sorted combustible" is not a fraction** — it counts as mixed/residual waste and does
  **not** contribute to the 70 %. Delivery for **reuse** does count, and must be recorded.

That last point is a small gift to a reclaimed-timber project: material sent to reuse is
counted as sorted.

## §9-9 — final report

> «For tiltak etter § 9-6 første ledd skal det utarbeides en sluttrapport som viser faktisk
> disponering av avfallet, fordelt på ulike avfallstyper og avfallsmengder.»

Same scope again — **§9-6 first paragraph**. Submitted to the municipality with the
application for **ferdigattest**, per **SAK10 §8-1(4)**, supported by weighing slips or
equivalent showing date, recipient, sender, waste type and quantity.

## §9-10 — wood-stove emissions

> «(1) Lukket ildsted for vedfyring skal utføres slik at det oppnås tilfredsstillende
> sikkerhet mot forurensning. Utslipp av partikler fra slikt ildsted skal ikke overstige
> verdiene gitt i Norsk Standard NS 3059:1994 …»

The binding text is a **performance reference to NS 3059:1994**. The numbers sit in the
guidance:

| Stove | Maximum particle emission |
|---|---|
| **with catalyst** | **5 g/kg** wood |
| **other technology** | **10 g/kg** wood |

Tested per **NS 3058:1994** at an accredited body. Appliances in scope, per **NS-EN
13240:2001**: space-heating stoves with or without water jacket, closed fireplace inserts,
and wood-burning cookers, with or without water jacket.

**Exempt from particle testing:** storing (heat-accumulating) stoves per **NS-EN
15250:2007**, wood-burning cookers with a limited firebox intended **only** for food
preparation, and boilers intended for a boiler room. Paragraph (2) allows an older
**heritage stove** where it is necessary to preserve a culturally historic interior.

For an emergency cabin in Norway a wood stove is close to certain, so this is not a
side provision. It also has a consequence that is easy to miss: the stove's tested **flue
gas temperature** sets the chimney's required class, and the guidance to §15-3(2) fixes
that at **T400 minimum** for solid fuel. Stove selection therefore determines chimney
selection, which determines the roof penetration — a design chain that runs from a
catalogue number to a roof detail.

## Applicability map for the mikrohus route

| Section | Status on this route | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **§9-1** | **applies** | admitted in full; qualitative duty, NS 3466:2009 programme |
| **§9-2** | **applies — and bites** | no manufacturer documentation route exists for reclaimed timber |
| **§9-3** | **applies — siting gate** | contaminated-ground investigation before permit; municipal tiltaksplan |
| **§9-4** | **applies — siting gate** | only if a designated nature type is present and the plan is silent; no size exemption |
| **§9-5** | **applies — and rewards the concept** | design for disassembly must be documented; fastening decides it |
| **§9-6** | **conditional on 10 tonnes** | area limbs unreachable at 18 m²; limb (d) is a mass switch, aggregated per (2) |
| **§9-7** | **NOT applicable — new build** | existing buildings only; but applies to any source building dismantled |
| **§9-8** | **follows §9-6** | 70 wt %, on-site sorting, "sorted combustible" does not count |
| **§9-9** | **follows §9-6** | final report with ferdigattest, SAK10 §8-1(4) |
| **§9-10** | **applies if a stove is fitted** | 5 / 10 g/kg; T400 chimney follows from the stove's flue-gas class |

## What this chapter changes

1. **§9-2 reframes C-08.** The question is not "is reclaimed timber allowed" but "by what
   documented route", and the regulation's own instruments all require a manufacturer.
   Testing or a professional assessment is the only path, and it should be scoped before
   the timber is bought.
2. **§9-3 is a second siting gate.** Chapter 7 asks whether nature threatens the building;
   §9-3 asks whether the ground threatens the occupants. Both are pre-design, both are
   plot-level, and neither is answered.
3. **The 10-tonne limb is a mass-dependent regulatory switch**, coupled to the reclaimed
   strategy through the aggregation rule in §9-6(2). It shares its unknown (C-07) with the
   lift plan.
4. **§9-5 is the chapter's gift.** Design for disassembly, documented, with fastening as
   the decisive detail — that is a description of what MiniHouse is trying to be, and it
   is now a requirement with a documentation duty attached.

## Limits

- No naturbase query, no ground investigation, no municipal plan check has been done.
  These are requirements, not findings about a plot.
- The §9-2 analysis is a reading of the guidance's compliance instruments. It is **not** a
  legal opinion that reclaimed timber is non-compliant, and not a substitute for the
  municipality's or the supplier's position.
- The 10-tonne limb is stated as a threshold; the cabin's waste tonnage is unknown and is
  not estimated here.
- No stove, chimney or product has been selected. No emission claim is made.
