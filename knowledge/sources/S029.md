## Why this pair matters more than its length

Chapter 2 has four short sections and chapter 4 has two. Between them they contain almost no
numbers that reach a drawing. They are nevertheless the chapters that decide **whether any of
this project's analysis is admissible**, and that is not a rhetorical point.

Every screening report in this repository — daylight, fire, room height, natural hazards,
envelope, section, options, installations — is an *analyse*. Chapter 2 says what an analysis
has to satisfy to count. Until now the project has been producing them under an assumed
standard of rigour. §2-2(3) states that standard explicitly, and it is stricter than
"the number looks right".

## §2-1 — the general duty, and the standards rule

> «(1) Det skal dokumenteres at kravene i forskriften er oppfylt i det ferdige byggverket.»
>
> «(2) Dokumentasjonen skal være skriftlig.»

Two sentences. The object of the duty is the **finished building**, not the intention, and
the form is **written**.

The guidance carries the operative content, and one part of it lands directly on an open
blocker:

- A relevant and valid **Norwegian Standard**, or an equivalent standard, may be used to
  document that requirements and pre-accepted performances are met.
- Where the regulation names no standard, it is the **ansvarlig prosjekterende** who must
  assess whether a standard is suitable and valid, and **that assessment must itself be
  documented**.
- **National Annexes must be applied** to meet Norwegian building rules — including where a
  pre-accepted performance is tied to a specific standard.
- The guidance distinguishes standards that **«skal»** be used (the requirement is concretised
  in the standard), standards that **«kan»** be used (in practice they must be, though
  deviation is allowed **without dispensation** if compliance is documented another way — and
  the guidance notes this is very demanding for structural safety), and standards that are
  **part of a pre-accepted performance** (they must be used).
- A standard cited **without a year** means the current version at any time.

**The National Annex sentence connects two blockers that were being tracked separately.**
S014 records that the Norwegian NA texts are paywalled. That was filed as a *design* blocker —
no γ or ψ values may be used. §2-1's guidance makes it a **documentation** blocker as well:
the Eurocode route cannot be documented as compliant without the NAs, so the structural
chapter cannot be closed on either count until they are obtained.

## §2-2 — the two routes, and the boundary that decides everything

> «(1) Kravene til ytelser som er gitt i forskriften skal oppfylles.»
>
> «(2) Der kravene til ytelser **ikke** er gitt i forskriften, skal oppfyllelse av
> funksjonskravene i forskriften dokumenteres enten a. ved bruk av preaksepterte ytelser,
> eller b. ved analyse som viser at ytelsene oppfyller funksjonskravene i forskriften.»

This is the most useful paragraph in either chapter, and its structure is easy to miss
because it reads like boilerplate. It is a **two-branch gate**:

```
Is a performance requirement (ytelse) given in the regulation?
├─ YES → §2-2(1): meet it. Analysis is not a substitute.
└─ NO  → §2-2(2): document the functional requirement, by
          (a) pre-accepted performance, or
          (b) analysis.
```

So the sentence that matters for this project is not "analysis is allowed" but **"analysis is
allowed only where no number was given"**. You can analyse your way *up to* a functional
requirement. You cannot analyse your way *past* a stated performance.

Applied to MiniHouse, that separates the open questions into two classes that have been
treated as one:

| Requirement | Given ytelse or function? | Is analysis available? |
|---|---|---|
| §14-3 U wall 0.22 / roof 0.18 / floor 0.18, 1.5 h⁻¹ | **given number** | **no** — meet it |
| §11-4(3) no. 4 **R 15** | **given number** | **no** — meet it |
| §12-7(2)(c) **2.2 m** | **given number** | **no** — meet it |
| §11-14 **0.86 m** | **given number** | **no** — meet it |
| §15-6(2) **0.90 m** | **given number** | **no** — meet it |
| §9-6 **10 tonnes** | **given number** | **no** — meet it |
| §7-2 **F2 = 1/200**, §7-3 **S2 = 1/1000** | **given class** | **no** — meet it |
| §12-7(2)(d) parts of a room may be lower | **function, no fraction** | **yes** — analysis is the instrument |
| §13-1(1) satisfactory air quality | **function, no quantity** | **yes** |
| §11-4(3) no. 6 whether the roof is decisive for stability | **function** | **yes** |
| §11-13(4) whether one escape window suffices | **interpretation of scope** | not analysis — a determination of which ytelse applies |
| §15-8(1) 100-year climate-adjusted stormwater | **given basis** | **no** — meet it |

Two consequences follow, and both are uncomfortable in a useful way.

**First, the room-height problem is an analysis and must be treated as one.** §12-7(2)(d)
permits lower parts and a sloped ceiling but states no required fraction. That is exactly a
functional requirement without a number, so §2-2(2)(b) analysis is the correct instrument —
and §2-2(3) then imposes conditions on it. The section solver is therefore not an informal
sanity check; it is the formal basis for a design decision, and it must carry the
documentation §2-2(3) demands.

**Second, the fire questions are not analyses at all.** Whether the roof is part of the main
load-bearing system, and whether one escape window satisfies §11-13(4) for a one-room cabin,
are questions about **which ytelse applies**. Analysis cannot answer them; a determination
by the responsible designer, agreed with the municipality, can. Filing them as "open
technical questions" has been accurate but incomplete — they are *classification* questions,
and they block the choice of ytelse rather than its verification.

## §2-2(3) — what makes an analysis admissible

> «(3) Dersom oppfyllelse av funksjonskravene i forskriften dokumenteres ved analyse, skal det
> påvises at den anvendte analysemetoden er egnet til og gyldig for formålet. Forutsetningene
> som legges til grunn skal være beskrevet og begrunnet. Analysen skal angi nødvendige
> sikkerhetsmarginer.»

Four demands, and they are a checklist any reviewer can apply:

1. the method is shown to be **suitable and valid for the purpose**;
2. the **assumptions** are described;
3. the assumptions are **justified** — description alone is not enough;
4. the analysis states the **necessary safety margins**.

This is why every screening report in this repository carries a "Limits" section, and it
upgrades that section from good practice to a **regulatory requirement**. A screen that lists
its exclusions is doing §2-2(3) work; a screen that does not is inadmissible however correct
its arithmetic.

The fourth demand is the one the project has handled least explicitly. The screens state
their *inputs* and *exclusions* well. They rarely state a **margin** as a number — the
transport screen's 4.00 m is a screening value, the 100 mm reserve in the R2 options is a
project choice, and the natural-hazard screen's 1.4 material factor is flagged unverified.
Those are three different kinds of margin presented in three different ways, and §2-2(3)
asks for one coherent statement of what margin is being relied on and why it is enough.

## §2-2(4) — the output must be a basis for detailed design

> «(4) Dokumentasjonen skal beskrive hvordan byggverket skal utformes og hvilke ytelser som er
> lagt til grunn. De fastsatte ytelsene skal gi et tilstrekkelig underlag for
> detaljprosjekteringen.»

The documentation must describe **how the building is to be designed** and **which
performances are assumed**, and those performances must give a **sufficient basis for detailed
design**.

This is the cleanest available description of where Fase 1 actually stands. The screens have
produced a great deal of §2-2(3)-quality analysis and, so far, **one conclusion that the basis
does not yet exist**: at 20° with a realistic envelope the section does not close under
4.00 m. That is not a failure of the work; it is §2-2(4) doing its job — the performances
assumed so far do not yet give a sufficient basis, so detailed design cannot start. R2 is the
step that produces the missing basis, and until one option is chosen, §2-2(4) is unsatisfied
by construction.

## §2-3 — the production basis

> «(1) Det skal dokumenteres at de prosjekterte løsningene og produktspesifikasjonene oppfyller
> de fastsatte ytelsene.»
>
> «(2) Det skal utarbeides et produksjonsunderlag som er tilstrekkelig for utførelsen.»

Two duties: show that the **designed solutions and product specifications** meet the fixed
performances, and produce a **produksjonsunderlag sufficient for execution**.

**The project has not reached this stage and should not pretend otherwise.** No product is
specified, so §2-3(1) has nothing to document yet. The relevance now is directional: §2-3(1)
is why the envelope screen's "generic λ, no products selected" caveat matters. A U-value
demonstrated with generic conductivity is not yet a demonstration that a *product
specification* meets §14-3 — it is a demonstration that a *thickness* could. The distinction
is the whole of §2-3(1).

## §2-4 — execution against the production basis

> «Det skal dokumenteres at utførelsen og valgte produkter er i samsvar med
> produksjonsunderlaget.»

One sentence, and it is the chain's last link: the built work and the chosen products must be
shown to match the produksjonsunderlag. It closes the loop opened by §2-2 — function →
performance → production basis → as-built.

For MiniHouse there is a wrinkle worth naming early. §2-4 compares execution against the
produksjonsunderlag, and a **transportable** building is executed once but *installed* many
times. Each installation has its own foundation, connections and site conditions that are not
in the produksjonsunderlag of the module. That gap is not covered by §2-4 as written, and it
is the same gap §4-1's FDV duty runs into from the other side.

## §4-1 — FDV documentation

> «(1) Ansvarlig prosjekterende og ansvarlig utførende skal, innenfor sitt ansvarsområde,
> framlegge den nødvendige dokumentasjonen for ansvarlig søker. Dokumentasjonen skal gi
> grunnlag for hvordan igangsetting, forvaltning, drift og vedlikehold av byggverket, tekniske
> installasjoner og anlegg skal utføres på en tilfredsstillende måte.»
>
> «(2) I tilfeller der slik dokumentasjon åpenbart er overflødig, bortfaller kravet.»

The guidance supplies the operational content:

- sufficient documentation must exist **at ferdigattest** as a basis for FDV;
- the regulation requires the **documentation**, not the management itself;
- it must be **ajour** and correspond to the building **as actually built** at handover;
- it must state the **premises, conditions and limitations** underlying the design, so the
  building is used within its permits and so future changes can be assessed;
- language: **Norwegian or another Scandinavian language**;
- the ansvarlig søker coordinates it and hands it to the owner **against a receipt**.

The general content list runs a–o (environmental follow-up plan, **brannkonsept**, façade
drawings, floor plans, representative sections, site plan, **structural calculations and
drawings of load-bearing parts**, **energy calculations**, fire-technical drawings, working
drawings, product data sheets, service agreements, basic data, public documents including the
ferdigattest, plan with provisions).

**For a residential building the guidance asks for something simpler**, and this is the list
that applies to MiniHouse:

- documentation of **products and surfaces** to be cleaned and maintained;
- **operation and service needs** for the technical installations;
- **instructions for use** of the dwelling;
- covering building products, sanitary installations, heating installations, ventilation,
  electrical installations, and fire alarm and extinguishing installations.

That is a short, concrete and achievable deliverable list — six installation categories plus
products, surfaces and instructions. It is worth writing down now, because it is the one part
of the whole compliance picture that is *finished* in scope and merely needs filling.

### The "small and simple" exemption does not fit this building

§4-1(2) drops the FDV duty where the documentation is **obviously superfluous**. The guidance
gives examples: **smaller garages, boathouses (naust), outhouses (uthus)** and similar small,
simple measures.

It is tempting to read an 18 m² cabin into that list. It does not belong there, and the
reason is structural rather than a matter of taste: **every example is an unheated,
unserviced, non-dwelling structure.** MiniHouse has a wood stove, a water installation, a
drainage installation, ventilation and a fire-detection system — five of the six categories
the residential FDV list names. A building with a combustion appliance and a wet room is not
a measure whose maintenance documentation is "obviously superfluous".

So the exemption should be recorded as **considered and rejected**, not left as an
unexamined possibility. That is the difference between a determination and an omission, and
this project has been careful about that distinction elsewhere.

### FDV is a living document for this building, not a handover file

Two sentences in the guidance combine into something specific to a transportable cabin: FDV
must be **ajour**, must match the building **as actually built**, and must state the
**premises, conditions and limitations** of the design.

For a building that is designed to be **re-sited**, every move changes the premises: ground
conditions, flood level, wind and snow exposure, foundation, service connections. A single
handover binder cannot stay *ajour* across that. The FDV obligation therefore argues for a
**structured, versioned file with a site-specific section re-issued at every deployment** —
which is a design decision about documentation architecture, not a filing preference, and it
should be decided before the first move rather than after it.

## §4-2 — the owner keeps it

> «Dokumentasjon for driftsfasen skal overleveres til og oppbevares av eier av byggverket.»

Single sentence, no paragraphs, no amendments. Two obligations on the **owner**: receive it,
and keep it. Nothing in the provision sets a retention period or a location, and this card
does not invent one.

## What this chapter pair changes

1. **§2-2(2) draws a line the project has been blurring.** Analysis is available only where
   the regulation states a function and no number. Every fixed ytelse — the U-values, R 15,
   2.2 m, 0.86 m, 0.90 m, 10 t, F2/S2, the 100-year basis — must simply be met. The
   room-height fraction is an analysis; the fire questions are **classification**, not
   analysis.
2. **§2-2(3) is the admissibility test for every screen in the repository**: method validity,
   described assumptions, *justified* assumptions, and stated safety margins. The "Limits"
   sections are now a regulatory requirement rather than good manners — and the **margin**
   demand is the one least well met so far, with three different kinds of margin presented
   three different ways.
3. **§2-2(4) explains the current state honestly.** The assumed performances do not yet give
   a sufficient basis for detailed design. R2 is the step that produces it; until an option is
   chosen, §2-2(4) is unsatisfied by construction.
4. **§2-3(1) makes "generic λ" a real caveat.** A U-value shown with generic conductivity
   demonstrates that a *thickness* works, not that a *product specification* does.
5. **§4-1(2) is a dead end and should be recorded as rejected.** The examples are all
   unheated, unserviced structures; a cabin with a stove and a wet room is not one.
6. **FDV must be a versioned, site-specific file** because it has to stay *ajour* and match
   the building as built across repeated re-siting. That is a documentation-architecture
   decision, and it is cheap now and expensive later.
7. **§2-1 ties the National Annex blocker to documentation.** Without the NAs the structural
   route cannot be documented as compliant, so S014 blocks design *and* compliance.
