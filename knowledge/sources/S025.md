# S025 · TEK17 Chapter 12 — layout and building parts, applicable to the mikrohus route

- **Publisher:** DIBK, Norway
- **Access:** free, official; retrieved 2026-11-17
- **Base index:** https://www.dibk.no/regelverk/byggteknisk-forskrift-tek17/12
- **Scope driver:** TEK17 §1-2(2)(d) (S020)
- **Sections retrieved:** §12-1, §12-5, §12-7, §12-11, §12-13, §12-14, §12-15, §12-17

## The scope list is the finding

§1-2(2)(d) admits only these chapter 12 provisions on the detached ≤30 m² / one-unit
holiday-home route:

- **§12-1** first paragraph
- **§12-5** in full
- **§12-7** first paragraph, and second paragraph **letters c and d only**
- **§12-11** first and second paragraphs
- **§12-13** first paragraph, and second paragraph **letter d only**
- **§12-14** first paragraph **letters a to d**, and fifth paragraph **letter b**
- **§12-15** in full
- **§12-17** in full

Everything else in chapter 12 is **exempt**. That is a much shorter list than a
first reading of the chapter suggests, and it removes most of the dimensional
rules people assume apply to a dwelling.

**Two consequences worth stating plainly.** First, **accessibility is out**: no
wheelchair turning circle, no 1.5 m manoeuvring space, no 0.86 m internal doors
*from chapter 12*. The 0.86 m that does apply comes from **§11-14** (escape route
free width), not from §12-13(2)(a). Second, **there is no minimum bathroom size and
no storage requirement** — but §13-15 wet-room rules still apply in full (S023), and
§12-9's absence does not remove the functional need.

## §12-1(1) — layout adapted to function

> «Byggverk skal ha planløsning tilpasset byggverkets funksjon.»

No numbers. The guidance notes that the requirement is largely concretised in the
rest of chapter 12 — which, on this route, is mostly exempt. In practice §12-1(1)
is a functional test: the layout must work for its intended use. It is the clause
that keeps the exemption from becoming a licence for an unusable plan.

§12-1(2) (universal design for public and work buildings) is outside the admitted
list and is irrelevant to a dwelling.

## §12-7(2)(c) — the real source of the 2.2 m room height

> «Frittliggende boligbygning inntil 30 m² BRA med én boenhet i én etasje uten
> kjeller som ikke på noe punkt overstiger en høyde på 4,5 m over bakken og
> fritidsbolig med én boenhet skal ha høyde minimum **2,2 m**.»

This is the legal basis for the 2.2 m recorded from DIBK's mikrohus guidance in
S018. It is worth separating the two facts:

- **§12-7(2)(a)** would require **2.4 m** for rooms in permanent occupancy — **exempt**.
- **§12-7(2)(c)** requires **2.2 m** for this building type — **applies**.

So MiniHouse has a single room-height figure of **2.2 m** for the whole cabin, not
2.4 m for habitable rooms and 2.2 m for the rest.

## §12-7(2)(d) — parts of a room may be lower, and this matters for the gable

> «Deler av et rom kan ha lavere høyde der dette ikke påvirker rommets tiltenkte
> funksjon.»

Pre-accepted solutions:

1. Parts of a room for permanent occupancy with a **horizontal ceiling** may be
   **minimum 2.2 m**, where this does not affect the room's intended function.
2. Parts of a room may have a **sloped ceiling lower than 2.2 m**.

**This corrects an overstatement in the earlier R1 envelope work.** That work
reported: at 1100 mm stack and 20°, the maximum eave is 2354 mm — «only 154 mm
above the 2200 mm mikrohus room-height allowance before build-up». The arithmetic
is right, but the framing implied the **eave** had to reach 2.2 m plus build-up.
Under §12-7(2)(d) and pre-accepted solution 2, a sloped ceiling **may go below
2.2 m in parts**, so the eave is **not** the room-height datum.

**But the relief is narrower than it first looks, and the numbers say so.** If
**full-width** 2.2 m is wanted, the eave must be 2.2 m plus the floor build-up, and
the 154 mm is exactly that allowance. `scripts/room_height_check.py` computes the
largest pitch that still delivers full-width 2.2 m:

| Stack | Floor 100 mm | Floor 150 mm | Floor 200 mm | Floor 250 mm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 900 mm | 28.1° | 26.6° | 25.0° | 23.4° |
| 1100 mm | 21.8° | **20.1°** | 18.4° | 16.7° |

So at **20° with a 1100 mm stack**, full-width 2.2 m survives a 150 mm floor
build-up with **0.1° to spare** and fails once the build-up passes roughly 200 mm.
The earlier warning was therefore closer to right than "relaxed" would suggest:
what (2)(d) buys is not free height but the **option** of accepting partial
coverage — 91.6 % of the width at a 200 mm floor and 82.4 % at 250 mm, which the
tables quantify. Pitches of 5–15° keep a wide margin; 20° is where the two limits
meet.

What the regulation does **not** say is how much of the floor must reach 2.2 m.
It says «tilpasset sin funksjon» (§12-7(1)) and «der dette ikke påvirker rommets
tiltenkte funksjon» (2)(d). That is a **functional judgement**, not a percentage.
It has to be made explicitly and documented — not quietly assumed in whichever
direction is convenient.

## §12-5 — safety in use

Single paragraph:

> «Byggverk skal ha en utforming som forebygger fare for skade på personer og husdyr
> ved sammenstøt eller fall.»

Pre-accepted performance relevant here:

- Protruding building parts and edges that could injure must be **marked and secured**.
- Floor finishes must not be so slippery, dry or wet, that there is a risk of falling.
- Level differences that could be hazardous must be secured or marked.
- **Level differences greater than 0.5 m must be secured with a guardrail or another
  safe method** so that fall injury is prevented.

The guidance also cross-refers headroom to §12-7 and glass to §12-17.

### The 0.5 m rule and the escape window — a real tension to record

The cabin floor will sit some height above ground on its chassis and supports. If
that height exceeds **0.5 m**, §12-5 requires the level difference to be secured
«med rekkverk eller på annen forsvarlig måte». An openable escape window at
900 mm sill is a controlled opening in exactly that level difference.

Two readings are possible: the escape window's own design is the «other safe
method», or a guardrail/restrictor is expected. §11-13(4) explicitly contemplates
escape windows in low buildings, and §12-17(3) states that child safety must not
prevent use as an escape route — so the regulation is aware of the tension. But
**it is not resolved here**. It becomes a real question only if the finished floor
is more than 0.5 m above ground; if the cabin sits low, there is no conflict.
Record it, ask it, do not assume the convenient answer.

## §12-11(1) and (2) — balcony and terrace

- (1) Balconies, terraces and similar must have satisfactory safety and usability.
- (2) **Level differences greater than 0.5 m must be secured with a guardrail** per §12-15.
- (3) — turning space for wheelchairs — is **exempt** on this route.

**Consequence:** any terrace or deck that travels with the cabin, or is built on
site, needs a guardrail once it is more than 0.5 m above ground. §12-15 then fixes
the height. The ChatGPT-derived plan assumed a removable terrace; that terrace is
now a guardrail question, not a free choice.

## §12-13(1) and (2)(d) — doors

- (1) Doors, gates and similar must be **easy to see and use**, and designed so they
  do not injure people, livestock or equipment.
- (2)(d) Doors in **saunas, cold rooms and freezer rooms** must open outward and be
  openable from inside without a key — not applicable unless such a room exists.

**The dimensional door rules do not apply.** §12-13(2)(a) (0.86 m clear width for
entrance and communication-route doors), (2)(b) (0.76 m internal), (2)(c) (0.86 m
universal) and (2)(e) (**2.0 m clear height**) are all outside the admitted list.

So the door width for MiniHouse comes from **§11-14** — 0.86 m free width in an
escape route for risk class 4 — and the door **height** has no fixed number from
chapter 12. §11-14 requires «tilstrekkelig … høyde», which is a functional test.
A 2050 mm leaf comfortably satisfies it; the point is that no 2.0 m figure may be
quoted from §12-13 as if it applied.

## §12-14(1)(a)–(d) and (5)(b) — stairs

Admitted:

- (a) safe delimitation and a **handrail on both sides**;
- (b) at least one handrail with top edge **0.8–0.9 m** above floor or step;
- (c) **uniform rise**, same riser height over the whole flight;
- (d) **going at least 0.25 m** on the walking line, and equal tread depth on
  straight flights;
- (5)(b) stairs, ladders or stepladders used **only for operation** of the building
  are exempt from the section — **unless they form part of the escape route**.

Not admitted: landings above 3.3 m, lighting, slip resistance, **width 0.90 m
(0.80 m inside a dwelling)**, **headroom 2.1 m (2.0 m inside a dwelling)**, and the
handrail detailing in (2)–(4).

**Consequence:** a stair or ladder to a sleeping loft is governed by handrails,
uniform rise and 0.25 m going — not by a width or headroom figure from chapter 12.
If the loft is not measurable space and the stair is operation-only, (5)(b) can
exempt it entirely, **but not if it is part of the escape route**. For a cabin where
someone sleeps in a loft, that last qualification is the one that decides it.

## §12-15 — guardrails (in full)

- (1) Guardrails must protect against falls and collision and be designed to
  **prevent climbing**.
- (2) In stairs and ramps: **minimum 0.9 m** above floor or step, including
  intermediate landings and return guardrails.
- (3) Balconies, terraces, tribunes, walkways and similar: **1.2 m** where the level
  difference exceeds **10.0 m**; **1.0 m** up to 10.0 m. Measured from the top of
  the deck.
- (4) Level differences **less than 3.0 m** may be secured by another safe method
  instead of a guardrail (guidance example: a permanently fixed planter of
  sufficient depth, height and design).
- (5) Openings in the guardrail: **maximum 0.10 m** up to a height of at least
  **0.75 m**; for guardrails over 1.0 m, openings ≤0.10 m up to at least 0.25 m
  below the top. Horizontal gap between a building element and an externally mounted
  guardrail: **maximum 0.05 m**.
- Glass panels used in a guardrail must both prevent injury on breakage **and**
  prevent falling through the glass.

**Consequence:** for a cabin terrace less than 10 m up, the guardrail is **1.0 m**
with gaps ≤0.10 m. The 3.0 m alternative in (4) is a trap for a cabin: it permits
another safeguard, but only where the level difference is under 3.0 m and the
alternative genuinely prevents falls.

## §12-17 — windows and glazing (in full)

- (1) Windows and other glazed areas that could injure on breaking must be secured
  against impact and fall **up to at least 0.8 m above floor**. The securing may be a
  **sill/parapet**, **safety glass**, or **another justifiable method**. No securing is
  needed above 0.8 m. Safety glass is specified per **NS 3510:2015**; glass is
  dimensioned for loads per §10-2.
- (1)(a) In a building with a dwelling unit: glazing facing **balcony, terrace and
  similar must always be secured**; and windows/glazing in an exterior wall must be
  secured **from 6.6 m** above terrain, measured to the lower edge.
- (1)(c) In entrance areas and communication routes, glazing must be secured **in the
  direction of travel** — applies to all building types.
- (2) Glazing in entrance areas and communication routes where collision is possible
  must be **contrast-marked, visible from both sides, at two heights with centres at
  0.9 m and 1.5 m** above floor; the door marking pattern must differ from nearby
  glazing.
- (3) Where children may stay, windows **from 3.3 m** above terrain need child safety;
  pre-accepted: a fitting preventing small children opening it, or a restrictor to
  ventilation position, with a bottom ventilation opening limited to **0.08 m** free
  width. **Child safety must not prevent use as an escape route.**
- (4) Cleaning and maintenance must be possible without danger. Up to **6.6 m** a
  ladder may be used; above that a window lift is needed. Fixed glazing cleaned from
  inside needs an openable side or top panel with a sill at least **0.8 m** high, or
  an equivalent safe solution. Self-cleaning glass is not a substitute.

**Consequences for the current aperture table:** all three sills are **at or above
900 mm**, so the 0.8 m securing rule is satisfied by the **parapet/sill** itself and
no safety glass is required on that ground. The 6.6 m exterior-wall threshold and
the 3.3 m child-safety threshold are both far above a 3.2 m cabin, so neither bites.
Contrast marking only arises if glazing sits in an entrance or communication route
in the direction of travel — for a single-room cabin with a direct external door,
that is unlikely but should be checked on the plan, especially for any full-height
glazing beside the door.

**One useful coincidence:** the escape window must have a sill **≤1.0 m**
(§11-13(4)) and glazing must be secured up to **0.8 m** (§12-17(1)). A sill between
0.8 and 1.0 m satisfies both with no safety glass — which is exactly where V-01
(900 mm) sits. A window designed with a sill below 800 mm would satisfy the escape
rule and *create* a safety-glass requirement. That is a design constraint worth
keeping in the window schedule.

## Chapter 7 — since retrieved

§1-2(2)(a) admits **chapters 1 to 7 in full**, and chapter 7 (*Sikkerhet mot
naturpåkjenninger*) was the next gap flagged by this card. It has since been
retrieved as **S026**: MiniHouse is flood class **F2 (1/200)** and landslide class
**S2 (1/1000)**, both independent of C-01, with a quick-clay regime that applies
below the marine limit even outside mapped hazard zones. See [S026](S026.md).

## What this chapter does not settle

- How much of the floor must reach 2.2 m — a functional judgement under §12-7(1)
  and (2)(d), to be documented, not assumed.
- Whether the escape window needs a guardrail or restrictor when the floor is more
  than 0.5 m above ground (§12-5 vs §11-13(4)).
- Whether a loft stair is «operation only» or part of the escape route, which decides
  whether §12-14(5)(b) exempts it.
- Whether any glazing sits in the direction of travel and needs contrast marking.

## Limits

- All values are requirements or pre-accepted performances as published on the
  official pages; they are not results for MiniHouse.
- The exemption list is read from §1-2(2)(d) as published. If C-01 lands on a
  route other than §1-2(2) — an ordinary dwelling, or a temporary building under
  §1-2(5) — the admitted set changes and most of chapter 12 returns.
- Nothing here is an accessibility assessment, a fire strategy or a sign-off.
