## Key principles

- Moisture is involved in almost all building envelope performance problems: rot, corrosion, mould (IAQ), staining.
- Moisture damage requires **four conditions**: (1) moisture available, (2) a route/path, (3) a force causing movement, (4) a susceptible material. Eliminating any one gives complete control; in practice control as many as possible.
- Moisture balance: wetting vs drying vs safe storage capacity. Accumulation occurs when wetting exceeds drying.
- Drying mechanisms: surface evaporation/wicking, vapour diffusion, convection (air movement), drainage, **intentional convection = ventilation drying**.
- Wetting sources for walls/roofs: 1. Rain (absorption, penetration, splash/drips); 2. Water vapour movement (diffusion, convection via air leaks); 3. Built-in moisture; 4. Ground (capillary wicking, gravity, diffusion).
- Ventilation drying via clear air spaces and vent holes top and bottom of a wall is important for vapour-impermeable claddings (metal panels, most roofing) and systems that retain rainwater — improves survivability of small rain leaks and condensation.

## Relevance to MiniHouse

Directly informs the cold-climate envelope design with reclaimed timber cladding: a vented rainscreen cavity, a continuous air barrier, correct vapour control layer placement, drainage paths, and avoiding built-in moisture in reclaimed timber. The four-condition test is a useful check for each moisture failure mode.
