- **Scope driver:** TEK17 §1-2(2) admits chapters **1–7, 9–11, 14, 15–17** on the mikrohus route (S020)

## Why these three chapters are carded together

They are admitted as a block by §1-2(2) and they share one property: **almost all of the
block is either not applicable or not yet designed.** Chapter 15 splits into a part that
binds the cabin, a part that binds the *site*, and a part that this design never triggers.
Chapter 16 is lift safety control, and there is no lift. Chapter 17 has a single section,
and it exempts this building type by definition.

The value of carding them is therefore mostly **negative space made explicit** — a record
of what has been checked and found not to apply, which is the difference between an
omission and a determination.

## Chapter 15 — the shape of the chapter

| Group | Sections | Outcome for MiniHouse |
|---|---|---|
| **Binds the cabin** | §15-1, §15-3, §15-4, §15-5, §15-6 | **applies unconditionally** |
| **Binds the site** | §15-7, §15-8 | applies to the **external networks**, which are plot infrastructure, not cabin design |
| **Never triggered** | §15-2, §15-9 … §15-15 | not engaged by this design |

One structural point deserves emphasis because it is easy to get wrong: **chapter 15
contains no exemption for small buildings anywhere.** §15-5, §15-6, §15-7 and §15-8 were
each checked, and each states no small-building or cabin exemption. §15-8 is explicit that
the only scope limits are natural runoff from undeveloped land and stormwater arriving
from *other* properties. So the usual assumption — that a cabin is too small to attract
installation rules — is false here.

## §15-1 — general heating and cooling

The general duty, in seven paragraphs. The ones that shape the cabin:

- **(3)(b)** sufficient distance between the installation and combustible material to
  prevent ignition; **(3)(c)** safety against injury from high surface temperature, with
  the guidance putting the burn-risk threshold at **approximately 45 °C**.
- **(5)(d)** the installation must suit the chimney's temperature class, and the guidance
  to §15-3(2) fixes **T400 minimum** for solid fuel per **NS-EN 1443:2019**.
- **(5)(e)** placement in a **fyrrom** unless the unit is intended for installation in
  another room. A certified cabin stove is designed for the living room, so no boiler
  room is required; the guidance confirms a boiler room is not needed where electricity is
  the only energy source.
- **(2)** closed systems must be **pressure-tested** before handover.
- **(7)** an open fireplace without doors needs a tight-closing flue damper.

Pre-accepted performance for (3)(b): the floor of combustible material under and in front
of a solid-fuel stove must be protected with a non-combustible slab or class **B-s1,d0
(In1)**; firewalls must be documented elements or a traditional fire wall. Guidance to
(5)(e) allows units up to **70 kW** outside a boiler room, and requires recirculation air
at least **2 m** above floor for warm-air units in workshop-type spaces.

**The 45 °C figure matters more than it looks.** A cabin of 18 m² puts the stove close to
everything — a bench, a bunk, a wall. A stove whose casing reaches 45 °C at the distance
the furniture allows is a design constraint on the plan, not just on the appliance.

## §15-2 — central heating: not triggered

Requires tightness at maximum pressure, over-pressure and over-temperature protection, and
sectioning with shut-off. Not engaged by a single stove or a single electric heater: a
central heating installation is a **distribution system** (water or air) serving the
building. A cabin with one stove and panel heaters does not have one.

## §15-3 — flue and chimney

> «(2) Røykgasser skal føres ut fra byggverket på en måte som ikke medfører fare for
> antennelse av byggverket og nabobyggverk.»

Five paragraphs, and the guidance supplies the numbers:

| Requirement | Value / rule |
|---|---|
| Minimum temperature class, solid fuel | **T400** per NS-EN 1443:2019 |
| Flue cross-section | **unchanged bottom to top** — no narrowing or widening |
| Masonry or cast chimney | must stand on **load-bearing construction of non-combustible material** |
| Crack detection | outer faces **sufficiently accessible**; pre-accepted: an element chimney needs **at least two free sides**, either papered (negligible tensile strength) or faced with brick tied by mortar |
| Movement | free movement relative to adjoining building parts |
| Cleaning | **satisfactory access for feiing** and for removing soot |

Two consequences for a timber cabin with a metal roof:

1. **The two-free-sides rule is a plan constraint.** An element chimney buried in a wall or
   boxed on three sides does not comply, because cracks could not be seen. On an 18 m² plan
   that means the chimney needs a position where two faces stay visible — which competes
   with the compact layout and with the stove's own clearance.
2. **The T400 class comes from the stove, not from the cabin.** Stove selection therefore
   fixes the chimney, which fixes the roof penetration. Add §9-10's particle limits
   (**5 g/kg** with catalyst, **10 g/kg** otherwise) and the stove is chosen against three
   independent criteria at once — emissions, flue-gas class, and clearances.

## §15-4 — heat pump and refrigeration

Requires that the installation cause no harm to person, environment, installation or
building; tightness and protection against abnormal operation; automatic control and
energy-economical operation; and **sectioning with gas and liquid shut-off valves**.
Paragraph (3) requires **gas detectors** and **emergency ventilation** in machine,
refrigeration and freezer rooms holding large quantities of refrigerant.

The guidance excludes **pure process installations** and **movable** heating and cooling
devices, so a permanently installed cabin heat pump is in scope — but the obligations scale
with charge. Sectioning is required for an evaporator or other pressure vessel holding more
than **30 kg**, and a domestic split unit is far below that, so paragraph (3) is not
triggered. Environmentally hazardous refrigerants fall under **produktforskriften chapters
6 and 6a**: charge must be monitorable and pressure relief must not blow off directly to
the surroundings. Reference frame is the **NS-EN 378** series.

Practical reading: a small air-to-air unit is compliant by being a certified product
correctly installed. The clause that matters is the **movable** exclusion — a heat pump
that travels with the cabin must still be fixed for transport, and a fixed installation is
in scope.

## §15-5 — internal water

The health-driven requirements, with numbers that land directly on an 18 m² plan:

| Requirement | Value |
|---|---|
| Scald, personal hygiene, users who cannot regulate | max **38 °C** at the tap |
| Scald, other buildings | max **55 °C** at the tap |
| Legionella growth range | **20–45 °C**, optimum 37 °C; killed above 60 °C |
| Circulating hot water | minimum **65 °C** |
| Air gap above a normal drain | **20 mm** |
| Air gap above health-hazardous waste or turbulent drain | **50 mm** |
| Air gap between different water qualities | **50 mm** |
| Pressure-reducing valve | required above **0.6 MPa (60 m VS)** |
| Tightness test | **1 MPa** max, and at least **0.1 MPa** above max operating pressure |

Paragraph (4) is the one that shapes the design: leakage must be **easy to detect** and must
**not damage** the installation or building parts. The pre-accepted routes are open piping
in a drained wet room, pipes in cabinets, pipes in shafts with visible leaks, or a
**pipe-in-pipe** system with a manifold in a watertight cabinet. And where a fixed tapping
point exists whose leakage would not reach a drain, an **automatic leak stopper** is
required — a moisture sensor that closes a valve.

Paragraph (5) requires a **stoppekran** before the first branch, easily accessible and
marked. On a cabin that is also a transportable object, that valve is the thing that lets
the system be drained for a move — so it serves §15-5(3)(c) frost protection and the
transport procedure at the same time.

## §15-6 — internal drainage, and the 0.90 m rule

> «(2) Utstyr som er fast tilknyttet avløpsnettet skal ha vannlås eller tilsvarende
> funksjon.»

Drainage must carry the water away at the rate it arrives, resist loads, be frost-protected
and be tight. Paragraph (2) requires a **water trap** on everything permanently connected,
to prevent backflow. Capacity is dimensioned per **NS 3055:1989**.

The pre-accepted performance is the geometric one:

> the water level in the **lowest-mounted water trap**, and in internal sumps and tanks,
> must lie **at least 0.90 m higher** than the internal top of the main pipe, measured at
> the branch on the main pipe.

**This is the constraint a chassis cabin fights.** Writing it as an inequality, with ground
level as datum:

```
h_ffl − d_trap + d_crown ≥ 900 mm
```

where `h_ffl` is the finished floor level above ground, `d_trap` the trap water level below
FFL, and `d_crown` the depth of the main pipe's internal top below ground. Rearranged, it
sets a **minimum burial depth**:

```
d_crown ≥ 900 + d_trap − h_ffl
```

A cabin standing on a chassis has a low `h_ffl` — perhaps 300–450 mm. At `h_ffl` = 400 and
`d_trap` = 60 the drain must be buried at least **560 mm** deep. That is not a detail: it
sets a floor on earthwork, and earthwork is exactly what a "light touch on the ground"
premise wants to avoid. It also puts §15-6 in tension with §7-1(2) (do not create or worsen
a hazard) and with §9-3 (do not disturb contaminated ground) — a deeper trench is more
ground disturbed.

The rule is avoidable only by changing the drainage concept: a **sealed tank** with a pump,
or a raised floor, changes which pipe is the "main" and what its crown depth is. That is a
concept decision, not a fitting choice, and it is not yet taken.

## §15-7 — external water supply

Applies to the network outside the building. Numbers:

| Requirement | Value |
|---|---|
| Pressure at connection point, built-up area | normally sufficient at **2.5 bar** |
| Internal pressure above which a reduction valve is needed | **6.0 bar** |
| Minimum pressure at the firewater outlet at design flow | **1.0 bar** |
| Minimum distance, building to external water pipe | **4 m** |
| "Small" service pipe (overbuilding case) | internal diameter up to **25 mm** |

Frost protection by insulation or heat cable; tightness tested per **NS-EN 805:2000**;
backflow protection per **NS-EN 1717:2000**; pipes under buildings in casing or culvert;
no two connection points to the mains (sprinkler excepted); diffusion-tight pipes where the
route crosses contaminated ground.

The **4 m** distance and the **1.0 bar** firewater figure both reach back into siting: the
first constrains where the cabin can stand relative to the service trench, the second
connects to §11-17's fire-service water requirement from S024.

## §15-8 — external drainage, stormwater and groundwater

> «(1) Løsninger for infiltrasjon, fordrøyning og avledning av overvann skal til sammen
> dimensjoneres for nedbør med klimajustert 100-års gjentaksintervall, så langt ikke annet
> er bestemt i arealplan.»

Five paragraphs, and one of them is a number that is easy to misread:

- **(1)** stormwater — infiltration, detention and diversion **together** — must be
  dimensioned for **climate-adjusted 100-year** rainfall, unless the area plan sets another
  basis. The guidance gives the three-step strategy (*treleddsstrategien*): infiltrate,
  detain, then divert to a safe recipient. IVF data from Norsk Klimaservicesenter; county
  climate profiles give the *klimapåslag*.
- **(2)** drainage water should be infiltrated or handled locally, without flooding at the
  design rain intensity.
- **(3)** the building must be protected against **flooding from high water level or
  overpressure in the sewer**, and no nuisance odour. Pre-accepted: the **0.90 m** trap
  rule again, for stormwater and combined sewers; where that height cannot be achieved,
  **backflow protection and pumping** are the compensating measures.
- **(4)** the network must be self-cleaning, inspectable, frost-protected and tight
  (**NS 3550:1986**, **NS-EN 1053:1995**); pipes under buildings in casing or culvert;
  minimum **4 m** from building to external sewer pipe.
- **(5)** unused service pipes must be disconnected.

**Do not conflate the two frequencies.** The **100-year** figure in §15-8(1) is a *design
capacity* for the drainage system. The **1/200** in §7-2 is a *safety class* for the
building against flood. They are different instruments and the ordering is coherent: the
drain is sized for the 1/100 rain, and the building must be safe at the 1/200 flood. A
reader who collapses them will either over-size the drain or under-protect the floor level.

Where §15-8(3) and §7-2 meet is the **floor level**. A cabin in an F2 area needs both a
flood-safe FFL and backflow protection on the drain — and §15-6's 0.90 m rule then pushes
the drain down while §7-2 pushes the floor up. The two requirements pull the same geometry
in opposite directions, and the gap between them is the design space.

## §15-9 … §15-15 — lifting devices: not applicable

Heis, heisrom og sjakt, fritt sikkerhetsrom, løfteplattformer, rulletrapp og rullende
fortau, trappeheiser, and the link between lifting devices and harmonised standards. A
one-storey 18 m² cabin has no lift, no platform, no escalator and no stair lift. **Not
applicable, and recorded as a determination rather than an omission.**

The word collision is worth naming once: TEK17's *løfteinnretning* is a **lifting device
for persons**, not the crane lifting the module. Nothing in §§15-9…15-15 governs the lift
described in ESTA GD25001-EN (S017).

## Chapter 16 — Sikkerhetskontroll av heis: not applicable

Chapter 16 is **safety control of lifts**, an administrative regime of requirements,
responsibilities, duties and measures for installations in the operational phase. It is not
a documentation chapter — documentation is **chapter 2** (oppfyllelse av krav) and
**chapter 4** (FDV), both also admitted by §1-2(2).

With no lift in the building, **chapter 16 is not applicable.** Recorded as a determination.

## Chapter 17 — Klima og livsløp: §17-1 does not reach this building

Chapter 17 has one section.

> «Ved oppføring og hovedombygging av **boligblokk og yrkesbygning** skal det utarbeides et
> klimagassregnskap basert på metoden i Norsk Standard NS 3720:2018 …»

The duty is defined by **building category**, and the guidance is explicit that
*boligblokk* means every dwelling that is **not** a *småhus* (§1-3). A detached one-unit
cabin is a småhus. **§17-1 does not apply** — and there is no area threshold to argue
about, because the exemption is categorical, not dimensional.

Where it would apply, the content is: modules **A1–A4, B2 and B4** minimum, plus
construction-site waste in **A5**; building parts 215/216, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26 at two-digit
NS 3451:2022 level; a **50-year** calculation period; **biogenic carbon excluded**; EPDs
preferred and generic data carrying a **25 %** surcharge.

**The irony is worth recording.** A project whose entire premise is material efficiency,
reuse and transportability sits outside the one climate-accounting requirement in the
regulation. Nothing prevents doing the NS 3720 calculation voluntarily, and for a cabin
built from reclaimed timber the biogenic-carbon exclusion is exactly the rule that would
make its best argument invisible. If the account is ever produced, it should be produced
with that limitation stated.

## What this changes

1. **The 0.90 m backflow rule is a siting and earthwork constraint, not a plumbing
   detail.** On a low chassis it forces a minimum drain depth of roughly 560 mm, which
   pulls against the light-touch premise, against §7-1(2) and against §9-3.
2. **§15-8(1)'s 100-year and §7-2's 1/200 must not be conflated.** One sizes the drain,
   the other sets the floor level, and they pull the same geometry in opposite directions.
3. **The stove is a three-criteria choice.** §9-10 emissions, §15-1(5)(d)/§15-3(2) T400,
   and §15-1(3)(b)/(c) clearances and 45 °C all bind at once, and the chimney's two free
   sides then constrain the plan.
4. **Chapters 16 and 17 close cleanly**, and so do §§15-2 and 15-9…15-15. Four
   non-applicabilities are now determinations with a stated basis rather than silent gaps.

## Limits

- No product, stove, chimney, heat pump or drainage concept has been selected.
- The 0.90 m inequality is a restatement of the pre-accepted performance; it is not a
  plumbing design, and the branch geometry on a real main may differ.
- §15-7 and §15-8 are **site** requirements and depend on whether the plot connects to
  municipal mains or runs a sealed tank — unknown until C-01 and C-06.
- Chapters 2 and 4 (documentation and FDV) are admitted by §1-2(2) and are **not carded
  here**; they remain an open retrieval item.
- Nothing here is an installation approval, a fire approval or a completion certificate.
