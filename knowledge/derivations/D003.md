# D003 · Sling geometry and four-leg distribution

Status: **verified** (2026-11-16). Script: [verify_rigging.py](verify_rigging.py). Gate D5 partial.

## Statement

For a sling leg at angle α measured from the horizontal carrying vertical component V: tension T = V/sin α and horizontal component H = V·cot α. Four legs do not imply W/4.

## Derivation

Resolving the leg tension into vertical and horizontal components: V = T sin α ⇒ T = V/sin α. H = T cos α = V cos α/sin α = V cot α.

## Verification

| α | T/V | H/V |
|---|---|---|
| 90° (vertical, spreader) | 1.000 | 0.000 |
| 60° | 1.155 | 0.577 |
| 45° | 1.414 | 1.000 |
| 30° | 2.000 | 1.732 |

Report's 3,000 kg hypothetical (W = 29.430 kN, perfect 4-leg sharing, V_leg = 7.3575 kN): at 60° T = 8.496 kN, H = 4.248 kN; at 30° T = 14.715 kN, H = 12.744 kN. The report quotes 8.50 and 14.72 — agreement within rounding.

## Four-leg indeterminacy

Rigid-body equilibrium provides three independent equations (ΣFz, ΣMx, ΣMy) for four unknown vertical leg forces → degree of indeterminacy 1. Individual forces depend on stiffness, leg-length tolerance, pre-tension, rigging geometry and CG. Two effective legs instead of four double V_leg (W/2 instead of W/4), so T at 60° rises from 8.496 to 16.991 kN in the hypothetical case. The design case must cover this pattern plus dynamics, with the factor fixed by the lifting specialist.

## Limits

Static, ideal geometry. No dynamics, wind, hook height, leg-length spread, crane radius or sling WLL included. Never use these numbers to select slings or a crane for the real module.
